Trustworthy AI perception in a control loop

What should an autonomous vehicle do when its AI perception becomes uncertain, and how should control be shared between the automation and the driver? This course answers that by building a small lane-keeping system, one idea at a time, that stays safe when its perception fails.

Elie Rouphael · Assumes basic linear algebra, probability and Python; no prior control theory or deep learning

The idea you prove to yourself

A neural network maps camera pixels to the lane offset and heading a controller needs. Inside the conditions it was trained on, it works. Outside them it can be confidently wrong, and in a closed loop a wrong estimate does not just sit on a screen: the controller acts on it and the car moves. The course compares three ways of using the same perception.

Naive AI

The perception drives the controller directly. No monitoring.

Hard handover

Switch to the driver when the network's reported uncertainty crosses a threshold.

Integrity-aware

Fuse epistemic uncertainty, a Kalman-filter consistency test and operational-domain membership into an integrity score, then share authority smoothly and slow down.

On an out-of-distribution curve the network's reported uncertainty barely moves, so the threshold never triggers. Only the model-based checks catch it. The capstone shows, with a controlled and statistically tested experiment, that using perception through a monitoring layer is safer than either trusting it or switching away from it.

Chapters

Each chapter isolates one idea, derives the mathematics, implements it from scratch in numpy, runs an experiment, and ends with exercises and references to the primary literature.

  1. 00 Introduction and Overview The closed-loop problem, why self-reported confidence is not enough, and the notation used throughout. You reproduce Reported uncertainty stays flat while NIS jumps from 1.3 to 13.6 out of distribution.
  2. 01 The vehicle model The kinematic bicycle from the no-slip constraints, Frenet error dynamics, discretisation, linearisation, controllability and observability. You reproduce Euler convergence order fits 1.00; the state is observable from the lateral offset alone.
  3. 02 LQR lane keeping LQR by dynamic programming, the discrete Riccati equation, Bryson's rule, curvature feed-forward and gain scheduling. You reproduce DARE residual of 7e-15 and the tracking-versus-effort cost frontier.
  4. 03 Perception and image rendering Pinhole projection, the ground-plane homography, blur and noise models, and a synthetic lane camera. You reproduce An out-of-distribution curve is invisible in the pixel statistics.
  5. 04 Uncertainty and deep ensembles Aleatoric versus epistemic uncertainty through the law of total variance, deep ensembles, calibration and proper scoring rules. You reproduce Ensemble NLL of -0.37 against 11.6 for a single model, and the case where uncertainty is not error.
  6. 05 Kalman filtering and the NIS consistency test The Kalman filter as recursive Bayes, innovation whiteness, the chi-squared NIS statistic and consistency tests. You reproduce White innovations and a mean NIS of 2.08 against the expected 2.
  7. 06 Operational design domain and the integrity score The operational design domain as set membership, three detectors, their fusion into an integrity score, ROC and detection delay. You reproduce Fused AUC of 0.96 against roughly 0.70 for any single detector.
  8. 07 Shared human-vehicle control Haptic shared control, the McRuer crossover driver model, workload, and the law that shares authority between driver and automation. You reproduce Stability with a delayed driver in the loop and integrity-aware conflict arbitration.
  9. 08 Capstone: the whole system Everything assembled into one closed loop and evaluated as a controlled experiment, with multi-seed statistics and a component ablation. You reproduce Proposed versus naive: -0.170 m lateral RMSE, 95% CI [-0.185, -0.157].
  10. 09 Advanced ideas and next steps CUSUM change detection and its average run length, conformal prediction with adaptive coverage, and a research roadmap. You reproduce CUSUM beats the single-sample gate on the detection-delay versus false-alarm trade-off.

Running it yourself

Every chapter page shows the notebook with its outputs, so you can read the whole course here. To run and modify the code, download the course (notebooks, the courselib reference library and the requirements) and run:

pip install -r requirements.txt
jupyter notebook            # then open notebooks/00_overview.ipynb

No GPU, deep-learning framework or simulator is needed: the course runs on numpy and matplotlib only. Work through the notebooks in order. Chapters 00 to 07 are self-contained; the capstone and the advanced chapter import courselib, the tidy version of everything built before.

What makes it rigorous

  • Derivations, not assertions. The Riccati recursion, the chi-squared distribution of the NIS, the total-variance decomposition and the conformal coverage bound are each worked out.
  • Every claim is run. Convergence orders, filter consistency, ROC and AUC, bootstrap confidence intervals and ablations are computed, not stated.
  • Primary sources. About 90 references across the ten chapters, from Kalman, Bar-Shalom, Anderson and Moore and McRuer to Lakshminarayanan, Vovk, Page and the driving-specific monitoring literature.
  • Honest limitations. The capstone states where the demonstrator is simplified (synthetic perception, kinematic plant, heuristic thresholds), and chapter 09 turns those limits into research directions.
