Disjunctive constraints and Big-M

Some constraints say "this or that": stay short of the crossing, or be beyond it while the bicycle is there. Each alternative is a half-line, easy on its own; their union has a hole in the middle, and that hole is what a convex optimiser cannot see. This chapter is about the word "or": why it breaks convexity, how a binary variable and a large constant write it as linear inequalities, what that constant costs, and why, with one decision, solving the two alternatives separately is the exact and better solver. The system is lab B1's longitudinal point mass, not the canonical cart.

Part 1

The forbidden interval, and why the union is nonconvex

The scene. A vehicle of length l_{\mathrm{car}} = 4.5 m drives along a road towards a crossing that a bicycle will occupy; the conflict region is w_{\mathrm{bike}} = 8 m wide, centred at d_{\mathrm{bike}} = 100 m. The vehicle's centre is d, so it occupies [d - l_{\mathrm{car}}/2,\ d + l_{\mathrm{car}}/2] and the region is [d_{\mathrm{bike}} - w_{\mathrm{bike}}/2,\ d_{\mathrm{bike}} + w_{\mathrm{bike}}/2]. Two ways not to overlap. Yield side: the front stays short of the near edge. Pass side: the rear has cleared the far edge. Hence

\boxed{d \le d_{\max} = d_{\mathrm{bike}} - \tfrac{l_{\mathrm{car}} + w_{\mathrm{bike}}}{2} = 93.75} \quad\text{or}\quad \boxed{d \ge d_{\min} = d_{\mathrm{bike}} + \tfrac{l_{\mathrm{car}} + w_{\mathrm{bike}}}{2} = 106.25},

and the forbidden centre positions form a gap of d_{\min} - d_{\max} = l_{\mathrm{car}} + w_{\mathrm{bike}} = 12.5 m.

Why the safe set is nonconvex. During the bicycle's presence the collision-free set is \mathcal S_{\mathrm{safe}} = (-\infty, d_{\max}] \cup [d_{\min}, \infty), the union of two half-lines. Take d^{(1)} = 92.75 and d^{(2)} = 107.25, both safe; their midpoint is 100, the middle of the crossing. A convex combination of safe points need not be safe, and that is the whole reason a logical decision appears: every tool of chapters A3 to A5 assumes the feasible set is convex, and a union of two convex pieces is not.

What is a disjunctive constraint?

In plain words: a requirement of the form "this or that", where each alternative is easy but the choice between them is not, because the set of allowed states is two separate pieces.

More precisely: a constraint x \in \mathcal A \cup \mathcal B with \mathcal A, \mathcal B convex and disjoint. With a binary variable s \in \{0, 1\} and a constant M large enough, it becomes two linear inequalities, d \le d_{\max} + M(1 - s) and d \ge d_{\min} - Ms, and the problem a mixed-integer quadratic program. With one binary variable it is solved exactly by solving the two convex alternatives and taking the cheaper feasible one.

Yield, pass
Stay short of the region; be beyond it, while the bicycle is present.
Big-M
A constant that switches an inequality off when its branch is not chosen.
MIQP
Quadratic objective, linear constraints, some integer variables.
Relaxation
The same problem with s \in [0, 1] instead of \{0, 1\}: a QP whose optimum bounds the MIQP's from below.
Part 2

One decision variable, two linear rows

Fix the strategy in advance and each problem is a convex QP: linear dynamics, linear state and input constraints, a convex quadratic objective, plus d_k \le d_{\max} during the bicycle's presence for yield, or d_k \ge d_{\min} for pass. The nonconvexity appears only when the optimiser is asked to choose between them.

The Big-M encoding. Let s = 1 mean yield and s = 0 pass. The symmetric form is

\boxed{d_k \le d_{\max} + M(1 - s), \qquad d_k \ge d_{\min} - Ms},

which relaxes the branch not chosen by M: for s = 1 the first row is the yield constraint and the second reads d_k \ge d_{\min} - M, void if M is large enough; for s = 0 the roles swap. With a quadratic objective and a binary s this is a mixed-integer quadratic program [2]. A compact one-sided variant, (1 - s)\, d_{\min} \le d_k \le d_{\max} + (1 - s) D with a large D, encodes the same logic with one constant and the knowledge that positions are non-negative.

Part 3

The smallest valid M, and what a huge one costs

Valid. "Void" means satisfied by every position the car could possibly be at. If the positions over the horizon lie in a known envelope [d_{\mathrm{low}}, d_{\mathrm{high}}], the switched-off rows need d_{\mathrm{high}} \le d_{\max} + M and d_{\mathrm{low}} \ge d_{\min} - M, so

\boxed{M \ge \max(d_{\mathrm{high}} - d_{\max},\ d_{\min} - d_{\mathrm{low}})}.

For positions in [0, 300] m that is M \ge \max(206.25, 106.25) = 206.25; for [0, 150] it is 106.25. Too small an M invalidates the formulation: a switched-off row would still cut feasible plans, and the hero turns the relaxed set orange to show it. The lab's round M = 5000 is valid, and 24 times larger than needed.

Why a very large M is not free. A mixed-integer solver does not enumerate; it solves the relaxation with s \in [0, 1], uses its optimum as a bound, and branches on s only where the bound is not conclusive. Drag the hero's s to 0.5: for any valid M the relaxed rows allow d \in [d_{\min} - M/2,\ d_{\max} + M/2], which contains the whole crossing, so the relaxation can place the car in the middle of the bicycle and report a cost that no real plan attains. The larger M, the looser the relaxation, the weaker the bound, the more branching; large constants also worsen the conditioning of the QP and make integrality tolerances troublesome. Production hybrid MPC derives the smallest valid bounds from the state envelope, as Exercise 2 does; the satellite course's chapter 09 meets the same weakness in a scheduling MILP.

Part 4

Two QPs instead of a MIQP

There is exactly one binary decision, so the mixed problem is

\min\{J^\star_{\mathrm{yield}},\ J^\star_{\mathrm{pass}}\},

with infeasible branches discarded: solve the yield QP, solve the pass QP, keep the cheaper feasible one. Transparent, solver-independent, and exactly equivalent to enumerating the binary variable, with no M anywhere. From lab B1's nominal state (60, 5) with N = 4 and \mu = 0.10, yield is feasible at cost 2582.56 and ends at (85.886, 8.924), while pass is infeasible, so yield is selected; the yield bound is not even active, since the closed-form reachable terminal positions are [74.11, 85.89] and neither bound is within reach, so the "decision" was made by the dynamics. With more grip, \mu = 0.15, from (30, 17) both branches are feasible, and only the weights decide: at R = 0.1 and R = 1 passing is cheaper, at R = 1000 yielding, which can coast, wins. The widget solves both branches live.

When enumeration stops scaling. With m independent binary decisions there are 2^m branches: 8 for three, 1024 for ten, a million for twenty. A mixed-integer solver's pruning, by the relaxation bounds, is what makes such problems tractable, and the quality of the bounds, hence the tightness of M, is what decides how much pruning happens. The rule: one or two decisions, enumerate; dozens, formulate with the smallest valid constants and hand the problem to a branch-and-bound solver.

Part 5

One binary per manoeuvre, not per step

If the bicycle occupies the region over several steps, say k = 4, 5, 6, the yield constraint must hold at every one of them, and so must the pass constraint; one strategy variable for the whole window commits to one topological manoeuvre: near side throughout, or far side throughout. Independent binaries s_k at every step would let the optimiser be yield-side at one conflict instant and pass-side at a later one, which physically means driving through the forbidden interval while the bicycle is there. From (0, 19) with the constraint enforced only at step 4 the plan is feasible and passes 78.7, 98.7, 118.7 m at steps 4, 5, 6, straight through the crossing; enforced over the window it is infeasible, as it should be. Logical variables must encode the manoeuvre-level logic, not individual inequalities. Lab B1 builds the window version and maps where it bites.

Part 6

The code behind it

The package writes the branch QPs of lab B1 without any M: the strategy is an argument, and only its rows differ.

advanced_mpc_lmpc/collision.py · solve_collision_strategy

def solve_collision_strategy(
    x0,
    strategy,
    *,
    N=4,
    Ts=1.0,
    d_bike=100.0,
    l_car=4.5,
    w_bike=8.0,
    conflict_steps=None,
    mu=0.10,
    g=9.81,
    v_max=32.0,
    v_ref=20.0,
    Qv=3.0,
    Ru=1.0,
    Pv=3.0,
    yield_boundary="geometric",
    terminal_stop=False,
    discretization="euler",
    warm=None,
):
    """
    Solve one continuous convex QP corresponding to one logical strategy.

    strategy:
        'yield' -> stay on the near side of the conflict region
        'pass'  -> clear the far side of the conflict region

    The default N=4, Ts=1, Qv=3, Ru=1, Pv=3, v_ref=20, mu=0.10,
    v_max=32 and Euler dynamics reproduce the numerical setup of the
    supplied workshop, except that the default yield boundary uses the
    geometrically collision-free d_max from the accompanying slides.

    Parameters
    ----------
    conflict_steps:
        Prediction steps (1,...,N) at which the conflict exclusion is
        enforced.  None means terminal step N only, matching the workshop's
        T=t_min special case.
    yield_boundary:
        'geometric'       -> d <= d_max from vehicle/bike geometry.
        'workshop_center' -> d <= d_bike, reproducing the looser modified
                             constraint visible in the supplied notebook.
    terminal_stop:
        If True, additionally enforce v_N=0 for the yield strategy.
        The supplied workshop has this constraint commented out.
    discretization:
        'euler' reproduces the supplied notebook; 'zoh' is an explicitly
        marked modeling extension.
    """
    x0 = np.asarray(x0, float).reshape(2)
    strategy = str(strategy).lower()

    if strategy not in {"yield", "pass"}:
        raise ValueError("strategy must be 'yield' or 'pass'")

    N = int(N)
    if N < 1:
        raise ValueError("N must be positive")

    A, B = vehicle_matrices(Ts, discretization)
    Sx, Su = prediction_matrices(A, B, N)

    d_max, d_min = conflict_bounds(d_bike, l_car, w_bike)
    if yield_boundary == "geometric":
        d_yield = d_max
    elif yield_boundary == "workshop_center":
        d_yield = float(d_bike)
    else:
        raise ValueError(
            "yield_boundary must be 'geometric' or 'workshop_center'"
        )

    d_pass = d_min

    if conflict_steps is None:
        conflict_steps = (N,)
    else:
        conflict_steps = tuple(sorted(set(int(k) for k in conflict_steps)))

    if any(k < 1 or k > N for k in conflict_steps):
        raise ValueError("conflict_steps must lie in {1,...,N}")

    # Initial state is a real state and must satisfy the speed bounds too.
    if x0[1] < -1e-10 or x0[1] > v_max + 1e-10:
        return _empty_solution(
            strategy, x0,
            message="initial speed violates [0,v_max]",
            conflict_steps=conflict_steps,
            d_yield=d_yield,
            d_pass=d_pass,
            terminal_stop=terminal_stop,
            discretization=discretization,
        )

    # Cost:
    #   sum_{k=0}^{N-1} Ru a_k^2
    # + sum_{k=0}^{N-1} Qv (v_k-v_ref)^2
    # + Pv (v_N-v_ref)^2
    #
    # x0 is fixed, so its stage term is an optimization constant.
    q_blocks = []
    for k in range(1, N + 1):
        speed_weight = Pv if k == N else Qv
        q_blocks.append(np.diag([0.0, speed_weight]))

    Qbar = block_diag(*q_blocks)
    Rbar = Ru * np.eye(N)

    xref_stack = np.tile(np.array([0.0, v_ref]), N)
    base = Sx @ x0 - xref_stack

    H = 2.0 * (Su.T @ Qbar @ Su + Rbar)
    f = 2.0 * Su.T @ Qbar @ base

    Aubs = []
    bubs = []

    # Predicted speed constraints 0 <= v_k <= v_max, k=1,...,N.
    for k in range(1, N + 1):
        idx = 2 * (k - 1) + 1
        row = Su[idx]
        offset = Sx[idx] @ x0

        Aubs.append(row)
        bubs.append(v_max - offset)

        Aubs.append(-row)
        bubs.append(offset)

    # Conflict exclusion at selected prediction steps.
    for k in conflict_steps:
        idx = 2 * (k - 1)
        row = Su[idx]
        offset = Sx[idx] @ x0

        if strategy == "yield":
            # d_k <= d_yield
            Aubs.append(row)
            bubs.append(d_yield - offset)
        else:
            # d_k >= d_pass  <=>  -d_k <= -d_pass
            Aubs.append(-row)
            bubs.append(-d_pass + offset)

    A_eq = None
    b_eq = None

    if terminal_stop and strategy == "yield":
        # v_N = 0
        idx = 2 * (N - 1) + 1
        A_eq = np.atleast_2d(Su[idx])
        b_eq = np.array([-Sx[idx] @ x0])

    amax = float(mu) * float(g)

    res = solve_qp(
        H,
        f,
        A_ub=np.asarray(Aubs, float),
        b_ub=np.asarray(bubs, float),
        A_eq=A_eq,
        b_eq=b_eq,
        lb=-amax * np.ones(N),
        ub= amax * np.ones(N),
        x0=warm,
        maxiter=1000,
    )

    U = np.asarray(res.x, float).reshape(N, 1)
    Xpred = (Sx @ x0 + Su @ res.x).reshape(N, 2)
    X = np.vstack([x0, Xpred])

    objective = _full_cost(
        X, U,
        v_ref=v_ref,
        Qv=Qv,
        Ru=Ru,
        Pv=Pv,
    )

    return CollisionSolution(
        strategy=strategy,
        success=bool(res.success),
        objective=objective if res.success else np.inf,
        X=X,
        U=U,
        message=res.message,
        max_violation=float(res.max_violation),
        conflict_steps=tuple(conflict_steps),
        d_yield=float(d_yield),
        d_pass=float(d_pass),
        terminal_stop=bool(terminal_stop),
        discretization=discretization,
    )

Read only the block headed "Conflict exclusion at selected prediction steps". For each conflict step k, idx picks the position row of the predicted state x_k = S_x x_0 + S_u U (chapter A2's matrices), so row is the position's dependence on the inputs and offset its free motion. Yield appends d_k \le d_{\mathrm{yield}} as row @ U <= d_yield - offset; pass appends d_k \ge d_{\mathrm{pass}} with the signs flipped. Everything above those lines, the speed-tracking cost and the speed bounds, is shared by both branches, and everything below hands one convex QP to solve_qp with the friction limit as input bounds. The conflict_steps argument is the window of Part 5; its default, the last step only, is the single-instant version.

advanced_mpc_lmpc/collision.py · choose_collision_strategy

def choose_collision_strategy(x0, **kwargs):
    """
    Enumerate the two logical strategies and choose the lower-cost feasible one.

    With exactly one binary decision s in {0,1}, this is mathematically
    equivalent to exhaustive enumeration of the mixed-integer alternatives:
        min{J_yield^*, J_pass^*}.
    """
    solutions = [
        solve_collision_strategy(x0, "yield", **kwargs),
        solve_collision_strategy(x0, "pass", **kwargs),
    ]

    feasible = [sol for sol in solutions if sol.success]

    if not feasible:
        return None, solutions

    return min(feasible, key=lambda sol: sol.objective), solutions

The enumeration of Part 4 in four lines: both branches, the feasible ones, the cheapest. The docstring says what the function is: with one binary, this equals the mixed-integer optimum. The widget above is the same function in the browser.

Part 7

Common pitfalls

  1. "The double-integrator dynamics make the problem convex." No. Each fixed strategy is a convex QP; the union "d \le d_{\max} or d \ge d_{\min}" is not. The logic, not the plant, creates the nonconvexity.
  2. "An enormous Big-M can only help." Too small invalidates the formulation; needlessly large weakens the relaxation, slows the search and degrades the numerics.
  3. "The relaxation's optimum is a plan." At a fractional s the relaxed rows allow the car inside the crossing; the relaxation gives a bound, not a trajectory.
  4. "One binary per step is more flexible." It is wrong: it allows a plan that is on the near side at one conflict instant and the far side at the next, through the bicycle.
Part 8

Check your understanding

Try to answer each question before opening it.

1. How are d_{\max} and d_{\min} derived?

By preventing overlap between the vehicle interval [d - l_{\mathrm{car}}/2, d + l_{\mathrm{car}}/2] and the conflict interval [d_{\mathrm{bike}} - w_{\mathrm{bike}}/2, d_{\mathrm{bike}} + w_{\mathrm{bike}}/2]. The two no-overlap cases give d \le d_{\mathrm{bike}} - (l_{\mathrm{car}} + w_{\mathrm{bike}})/2 or d \ge d_{\mathrm{bike}} + (l_{\mathrm{car}} + w_{\mathrm{bike}})/2.

2. Why is the collision-free set nonconvex?

It is a union of two separated half-lines. Two safe points on opposite sides have an unsafe midpoint inside the forbidden interval.

3. What do the two Big-M rows do at s = 1 and at s = 0?

At s = 1 the first row is the yield constraint and the second is void; at s = 0 the second is the pass constraint and the first is void. "Void" requires M to cover the whole position envelope.

4. What is the smallest valid M, and what happens below it?

\max(d_{\mathrm{high}} - d_{\max}, d_{\min} - d_{\mathrm{low}}) for positions in [d_{\mathrm{low}}, d_{\mathrm{high}}]; below it a switched-off row still excludes feasible plans.

5. Why can we solve two QPs instead of a MIQP here?

There is one binary decision. Enumerating s = 0 and s = 1 gives exactly the minimum of the two branch-optimal costs, with no M and no relaxation.

6. What is the main danger of a large Big-M in a mixed-integer solver?

It weakens the continuous relaxation, whose bound is what lets branch-and-bound prune; a weaker bound means more branching, and large constants also hurt conditioning and tolerances.

7. Why one binary for the whole occupancy window?

Because the decision is a manoeuvre, near side or far side throughout; per-step binaries would permit crossing the forbidden interval between two conflict instants.

Part 9

Exercises

Each exercise has a hint and a solution; the code runs with the course package installed. Try first, then peek.

  1. Conflict geometry. Derive d_{\max} and d_{\min} from interval non-overlap, then show d_{\min} - d_{\max} = l_{\mathrm{car}} + w_{\mathrm{bike}}.

    Hint

    Two intervals do not overlap when one ends before the other begins.

    Solution

    No overlap means d + l_{\mathrm{car}}/2 \le d_{\mathrm{bike}} - w_{\mathrm{bike}}/2 or d - l_{\mathrm{car}}/2 \ge d_{\mathrm{bike}} + w_{\mathrm{bike}}/2, which rearrange to d \le d_{\max} and d \ge d_{\min}. Subtracting, d_{\min} - d_{\max} = 2 \cdot (l_{\mathrm{car}} + w_{\mathrm{bike}})/2 = 12.5 m. conflict_bounds() returns the pair.

  2. Big-M. Starting from s = 1 for yield and s = 0 for pass, derive the symmetric Big-M constraints, and for a known envelope d \in [d_{\mathrm{low}}, d_{\mathrm{high}}] derive valid lower bounds on M. Evaluate them for [0, 300] and [0, 1000].

    Hint

    The relaxed inequality must be satisfied by every position in the envelope.

    Solution

    Yield must hold when s = 1 and be void when s = 0: d_k \le d_{\max} + M(1 - s). Pass must hold when s = 0 and be void when s = 1: d_k \ge d_{\min} - Ms. "Void" means satisfied by every d \in [d_{\mathrm{low}}, d_{\mathrm{high}}]: d_{\mathrm{high}} \le d_{\max} + M and d_{\mathrm{low}} \ge d_{\min} - M, so M \ge \max(d_{\mathrm{high}} - d_{\max},\ d_{\min} - d_{\mathrm{low}}). For [0, 300] that is \max(206.25, 106.25) = 206.25; for [0, 1000] it is 906.25.

  3. The relaxation. With M = 206.25, write the relaxed set of positions allowed at s = 0.25, 0.5 and 0.75. At which s does the relaxed set first contain the whole crossing?

    Hint

    The set is [d_{\min} - Ms,\ d_{\max} + M(1 - s)].

    Solution

    [54.7, 248.4], [3.1, 196.9] and [0, 145.3] (clipped at the road's start). The crossing [93.75, 106.25] is contained as soon as d_{\min} - Ms \le 93.75 and d_{\max} + M(1 - s) \ge 106.25, that is s \ge 0.061 and s \le 0.939: almost any fractional s lets the relaxation sit in the crossing, which is why its bound is weak. The numbers script prints the five sets.

  4. Weights against feasibility. From (30, 17) with \mu = 0.15, solve both branches for R = 0.1, 1 and 1000. Does feasibility change? Does the choice?

    Hint

    choose_collision_strategy with mu=0.15 and Ru varied; the widget's second and third presets.

    Solution

    Both branches are feasible every time, with yield costs 220.80, 226.88, 1563.44 and pass costs 36.69, 41.76, 5165.38: the choice is pass, pass, yield. The weights change which feasible plan is cheapest and never whether a plan exists; feasibility is set by the dynamics and the constraints alone.

    for Ru in (0.1, 1.0, 1000.0):
    best, sols = choose_collision_strategy(np.array([30.0, 17.0]), N=4, mu=0.15, Ru=Ru)
    print(Ru, [s.success for s in sols], [round(s.objective, 2) for s in sols], best.strategy)

  5. Build it: the enumeration. Write your own choose(x0) that calls solve_collision_strategy for both branches and returns the cheaper feasible one, then count, on the grid d_0 = 0, 5, \dots, 95 and v_0 = 0, 1, \dots, 32, how many states have no feasible branch at \mu = 0.10.

    Hint

    660 states, 1320 QPs; strategy_grid does the same and is the check.

    Solution

    Seven states have no feasible branch, along the line d_0 + 4v_0 \approx 100: too fast to stop short, too slow to clear. strategy_grid returns the same count; lab B1 draws the map and repeats it at lower friction.

    def choose(x0):
    sols = [solve_collision_strategy(x0, s, N=4, mu=0.10) for s in ("yield", "pass")]
    feasible = [s for s in sols if s.success]
    return min(feasible, key=lambda s: s.objective) if feasible else None
none = sum(choose(np.array([d, v])) is None for d in range(0, 100, 5) for v in range(0, 33))
count, _ = strategy_grid(np.arange(0, 100, 5), np.arange(0, 33), N=4, mu=0.10)
print(none, (count == 0).sum())

  6. More decisions. Two bicycles cross the road 60 m apart, each with its own occupancy window. How many manoeuvres are there, and which combination needs an extra check before it is called feasible?

    Hint

    Before or after each; think about the gap between "after the first" and "before the second".

    Solution

    Two binaries, four combinations: before both, after both, after the first and before the second, and before the first and after the second, the last being impossible since the road is one-dimensional. "After the first and before the second" requires the car to fit between the two forbidden intervals during the overlap of the windows, which the two rows enforce automatically if both windows are encoded; with three bicycles the count is eight, and enumeration is still cheaper than a mixed-integer solver, which is where Part 4's rule of thumb starts to turn.

Part 10

The chapter on one page

  1. Geometry: safe centre positions are d \le 93.75 or d \ge 106.25, a nonconvex union with a 12.5 m gap; the midpoint of two safe points is unsafe.
  2. One binary: d_k \le d_{\max} + M(1 - s) and d_k \ge d_{\min} - Ms encode the disjunction; the problem becomes an MIQP.
  3. The smallest valid M is \max(d_{\mathrm{high}} - d_{\max}, d_{\min} - d_{\mathrm{low}}): 206.25 for positions within 300 m; 5000 is valid and 24 times too large.
  4. A huge M costs: at a fractional s the relaxation allows the car inside the crossing, so its bound is weak and branch-and-bound prunes less.
  5. Enumerate: with one decision, two QPs are the exact solver; from (60, 5) only yield is feasible, cost 2582.56; from (30, 17) with more grip the weights pick the branch.
  6. One binary per manoeuvre: per-step binaries would drive through the crossing between two conflict instants.

The vocabulary, in one table

Word Meaning
Conflict region, forbidden interval Where the bicycle will be; the centre positions that would overlap it.
Disjunctive constraint "Either here or there": a nonconvex union of convex sets.
Yield, pass Stay short of the region; be beyond it, during the occupancy.
Big-M, MIQP A large constant switching inequalities off; the resulting mixed-integer program.
Relaxation The binary allowed to be fractional; its optimum is a bound.
Enumeration Solving every branch as a convex problem and keeping the cheapest feasible one.
