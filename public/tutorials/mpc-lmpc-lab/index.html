Advanced MPC and Learning MPC

Model predictive control solves an optimisation problem at every sample. This course does not treat that optimiser as a black box: for every controller it asks what the decision variables, objective and constraints are, what makes the problem feasible at the next step, why the closed loop is stable, and which assumptions a real system would break. Part A teaches the concepts one at a time, each on the same small system, with the function that computes it read line by line. Part B assembles them into complete designs, from a collision decision to Learning MPC, which learns its terminal ingredients from its own successful executions, and to the connection with dynamic programming and reinforcement learning.

Elie Rouphael · Assumes linear algebra, basic control (state-space models, stability) and Python

How to read a chapter. Every concept chapter has the same shape: a live demo under the title, the idea in plain words and then precisely, the function of the course package that computes it read line by line, the pitfalls, questions with folded answers, exercises whose solutions run, and the chapter on one page. Every number quoted on a page is printed by that chapter's script, code/<chapter>_numbers.py, and the browser demos use the same algorithms as the package.

How to study each controller. Always find four layers. The model: what are the state x, the input u and the dynamics f? The optimisation: what are the decision variables, the objective and the constraints? The guarantees: what gives feasibility, safety or stability? The limitations: which assumptions would fail on a real platform? The labs are built around these four questions, and they are a good structure for reviewing any controller design.

Part A · Concepts

One idea per chapter, each on the same constrained double integrator: what the idea says in plain words and precisely, a live demo, the function of the course package that computes it read line by line, exercises whose solutions run, and a script that reproduces every number on the page.

  1. A1 State-space models and the double integrator States, inputs and dynamics, the four model classes, the course's double integrator from Newton's law with its two discretisations, and why the plant and the prediction model are different things. You find Held accelerations of 1, 1, 0, −1, −1 take the cart from (−3, 0) to (3, 0) in five moves inside its box; the same moves at half size for twice as long leave the box at step 7. Euler's position lags the exact one by Tsv/2, a whole metre at a one-second sample and a 2 m/s velocity.
  2. A2 Prediction matrices and condensing The stacked prediction identity, the condensed quadratic program it produces, and the sparse form that keeps the states as variables.
  3. A3 Convexity, quadratic programs and the KKT conditions Convex sets and functions, the Lagrangian, duality, the four KKT conditions, and multipliers as the price of a constraint.
  4. A4 Solving a QP you can read The course's own interior-point solver, sixty lines of NumPy read line by line, against SciPy's SLSQP on the course's problems.
  5. A5 The finite-horizon problem Constrained finite-time optimal control as one QP, hard and soft constraints, a terminal equality, and what breaks when the model is nonlinear.
  6. A6 Receding horizon: from a plan to feedback The receding-horizon law, the four properties that must be kept apart, and the horizon-one pathology.
  7. A7 Lyapunov stability and LQR Schur stability, the discrete Lyapunov condition, the infinite-horizon LQR, the Riccati equation and the one identity the whole stability theory rests on.
  8. A8 Polytopes and set operations H- and V-representations, membership and redundancy, intersections, images, preimages, Minkowski sums and projection.
  9. A9 Pre, Post and controllable sets The forward reachable set, the controlled predecessor as a projection, Fourier–Motzkin elimination, and the N-step controllable sets.
  10. A10 Positive and control invariance Two definitions that differ by a quantifier, the two fixed-point iterations that compute them, and why the LQR's invariant set is so much smaller.
  11. A11 Terminal ingredients and the stability theorem The shifted sequence, the three terminal conditions, the Lyapunov argument, the zero terminal constraint against an invariant region, and the horizon as a lever.
  12. A12 Sampled and convex safe sets What a successful run certifies, the sampled safe set, its convex hull with barycentric coordinates, and the invariance proof that uses linearity exactly once.
  13. A13 The learned terminal value and the LMPC problem Realised costs-to-go, their convex extension by a linear program, the decrease it satisfies, and the Learning MPC problem as one QP.
  14. A14 Bellman: dynamic programming, value and policy iteration The principle of optimality, the backward recursion, the Riccati recursion as its linear-quadratic case, the Bellman operator's contraction, and policy iteration.
  15. A15 Disjunctive constraints and Big-M Why "this or that" is nonconvex, one binary variable, the Big-M encoding and its pitfalls, and when enumerating the branches is the better solver.

Part B · Labs

The concepts assembled into complete designs: a collision-avoidance decision, Learning MPC on a repeated task, minimum-time racing, local models learned from laps, the dynamic-programming view that connects MPC to reinforcement learning, and one design checked end to end.

  1. B1 Vehicle–bike collision avoidance A yield-or-pass decision as two convex QPs, friction-limited braking, closed-form reachability and feasibility maps over initial conditions.
  2. B2 Learning MPC on a repeated task A short-horizon MPC produces a poor first run; Learning MPC learns the terminal ingredients from it and improves, iteration after iteration, with every guarantee checked.
  3. B3 Frenet coordinates and minimum-time racing Curvilinear coordinates, the progress rate, space as the independent variable, and an exact minimum-time speed planner along a fixed centreline.
  4. B4 Data-driven local models for racing A dynamic bicycle model, kernel-weighted local regression of its velocities from recorded laps, exact Frenet rows, and a time-varying prediction model validated on a lap it never saw.
  5. B5 MPC, Learning MPC and reinforcement learning MPC as truncated dynamic programming, LMPC as a learned upper bound of the value, tabular Q-learning on the same lattice, and what the contraction no longer covers.
  6. B6 Capstone: one design, end to end One MPC design followed from the terminal ingredients to a grid certificate of every guarantee, the same controller stripped of them, Learning MPC from a poor first run, and the engineering questions outside the theorems.

Running it yourself

Every chapter page runs its demos in the browser and quotes numbers computed in Python. To reproduce them, download the course: the advanced_mpc_lmpc package with its tests, the lab notebooks, and in code/ one script per chapter that prints every number quoted on that chapter's page. Then, from the unzipped folder:

pip install -r requirements.txt
python -m pytest                      # the package's tests
python code/a07_numbers.py            # every number on chapter A7's page
jupyter lab                           # the lab notebooks, in notebooks/

No commercial solver is needed: the package's quadratic programs are solved by its own interior-point method, a hundred lines of NumPy that chapter A4 reads line by line. Each script runs in seconds; the lab notebooks in under a minute each.
