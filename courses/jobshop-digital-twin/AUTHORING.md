## Form

Write in the course Markdown dialect documented at the top of `scripts/course_md.py`. Follow the model chapter's
structure:

- **Front matter**: `lede` (3–5 sentences: the question the chapter answers and what the reader will be able to do),
  `needs`, `build` (one line for the index card), `result` (the "You find" line: 2–3 concrete findings with numbers,
  all printed by your numbers script), `parts` (one `{id, d}` per `##` section, in order, ids matching the
  `## Title {#id}` anchors exactly), optional `css`.
- **`::: hero`**: one live demo under the title that makes the chapter's idea visible and playable.
- **Sections** (`## Title {#id}`), typically 8–11: the idea in plain words with a concrete example; the precise
  definitions (`::: define` boxes); the mathematics, derived step by step (no step skipped that a careful reader
  could not fill in); a code walk of the repository lines that implement it (`::: code jobshop/<file>.py:<function>`;
  paths are relative to the repository root, which is the course's `package_root`); pitfalls; `:::: qa` with 6–8
  check-your-understanding questions and folded answers; `::::: exercises` with 4–6 exercises, each with
  `::: hint` and `::: solution` (at least two exercises are pen-and-paper derivations or proofs, at least one is a
  coding exercise against the repository); a summary with the chapter's vocabulary and `::: refs`.
- **Length**: 4,000–6,500 words of text, plus demos.
- **Links**: `[[a02]]`, `[[c01#circuit|the circuit constraint]]`, `[[ref:3]]`. Respect the other chapters' scopes in
  `course.yaml`: introduce your own idea fully, refer back to earlier chapters for what they teach, and point forward
  rather than teaching another chapter's idea. Chapter ids and slugs are fixed; anchors inside other chapters may not
  exist yet, so link to other chapters without `#anchor` unless the anchor is your own.

## Pedagogy (non-negotiable)

1. **Plain words first, then precise.** Every concept appears first in everyday language with a concrete instance
   (the paint machine, a two-job example, a real number from the workshop), then as a definition, then as mathematics.
2. **No unexplained jargon.** Define every term at first use. Use the plain method names of `notation.md` with the
   abbreviation in brackets at first use.
3. **Derive, don't assert.** When a result is a theorem (SPT optimality, NP-hardness, the telescoping reward, the
   variance of a paired difference), state it, prove it or sketch the proof faithfully, and cite the source. When you
   only cite a result, say so.
4. **Small numbers the reader can check by hand**, then the real workshop.
5. **Honesty.** Everything is synthetic; say what a simplification costs. Never overclaim (the DQN agent does not beat
   the best rule; the AGVs are not optimised; gaps are against proven bounds).
