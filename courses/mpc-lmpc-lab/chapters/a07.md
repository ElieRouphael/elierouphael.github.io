## Stable in discrete time: Schur, not Hurwitz {#schur}

**Continuous and discrete time test differently.** For $\dot x = Ax$, asymptotic stability requires $A$ to be Hurwitz: every eigenvalue has negative real part. For $x_{k+1} = Ax_k$ the solution is $x_k = A^k x_0$, which decays for every start exactly when every eigenvalue has modulus below one. $A$ must be Schur:

$$\boxed{\rho(A) < 1}, \qquad \rho(A) = \max_i |\lambda_i(A)| .$$

The canonical cart is neither: both eigenvalues of $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ equal 1, and $A^{10} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 10 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, so a coasting cart keeps its velocity and its position grows linearly. Stability has to be made by feedback. With $u = -Kx$ the closed loop is $x^+ = (A - BK)x$, and the question becomes whether $A_{\mathrm{cl}} = A - BK$ is Schur.

::: define What is a Lyapunov function?
**In plain words:** a quantity, like an energy, that is positive everywhere except at the target and that the closed loop can only decrease. If such a quantity exists the state cannot wander off, because wandering would raise it.

**More precisely:** for $x^+ = F(x)$ with $F(0) = 0$, a function $V$ with $V(0) = 0$, $V(x) > 0$ for $x \ne 0$ and $V(F(x)) - V(x) < 0$ for $x \ne 0$. Its existence proves asymptotic stability of the origin, and for linear systems with a quadratic $V = x^\top Px$ the three conditions become two matrix inequalities, $P \succ 0$ and $A_{\mathrm{cl}}^\top P A_{\mathrm{cl}} - P \prec 0$. Chapter [[a11]] uses the optimal value $J_N^\star$ of the MPC problem as a Lyapunov function; this chapter builds the quadratic one it borrows its terminal term from.

- Positive definite $P \succ 0$ :: $x^\top Px > 0$ for every $x \ne 0$; equivalently, every eigenvalue of the symmetric $P$ is positive.
- Decrease :: $V(x^+) - V(x) = x^\top (A_{\mathrm{cl}}^\top P A_{\mathrm{cl}} - P)\, x$, negative for all $x \ne 0$ when the matrix in the middle is negative definite.
- Exponential :: With a quadratic $V$ the decrease is by a fixed fraction at least, so $\lVert x_k \rVert \le c\,\alpha^k \lVert x_0 \rVert$ for some $c > 0$ and $\alpha < 1$.
:::

## A function the loop can only decrease {#lyapunov}

**The discrete Lyapunov condition.** With $V(x) = x^\top Px$ and $P \succ 0$, one step changes $V$ by

$$\Delta V = V(x_{k+1}) - V(x_k) = x_k^\top \big( A_{\mathrm{cl}}^\top P A_{\mathrm{cl}} - P \big)\, x_k .$$

If some $P \succ 0$ makes $A_{\mathrm{cl}}^\top P A_{\mathrm{cl}} - P \prec 0$, the origin is asymptotically stable [[ref:3]], and in fact exponentially: $V$ falls by at least the fraction $\lambda_{\min}(-\Delta) / \lambda_{\max}(P)$ at every step.

**The converse, and how to find $P$.** For a Schur matrix the certificate always exists: pick any $W \succ 0$ and solve the discrete Lyapunov equation $A_{\mathrm{cl}}^\top P A_{\mathrm{cl}} - P = -W$, a linear system in the entries of $P$ with a unique positive-definite solution. Conversely, if $A_{\mathrm{cl}}$ is not Schur, no positive-definite solution exists. The widget solves the equation for $W = I$ and a hand-chosen gain: for $K = (0.5, 1)$ the closed-loop eigenvalues are $0.375 \pm 0.331i$, spectral radius 0.5, and $P = \begin{pmatrix} 2.083 & 0.625 \\ 0.625 & 1.521 \end{pmatrix}$ with eigenvalues 1.117 and 2.487. Along the loop from $(-3.6, 2.4)$ the value falls $24.96, 6.24, 0.75, 0.137, \dots$, by varying ratios, since this $P$ was not built from the cost the loop minimises. Push the gain until an eigenvalue leaves the unit circle and watch the certificate fail.

The eigenvalues decide stability; the Lyapunov function certifies it in a form that survives constraints, nonlinearity and optimisation, which the eigenvalues do not. That is why the MPC theory of chapter [[a11]] is written in terms of a decreasing function and never mentions eigenvalues.

## The infinite-horizon LQR and the Riccati equation {#lqr}

**The problem.** For $x_{k+1} = Ax_k + Bu_k$ and the cost

$$J_\infty = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \big( x_k^\top Q x_k + u_k^\top R u_k \big), \qquad Q \succeq 0,\ R \succ 0,$$

under the usual stabilisability and detectability assumptions, the optimal input is a stationary linear feedback $u = -Kx$ with

$$K = (R + B^\top P B)^{-1} B^\top P A, \qquad \boxed{P = A^\top P A - A^\top P B (R + B^\top P B)^{-1} B^\top P A + Q},$$
%narrow \begin{aligned} K &= (R + B^\top P B)^{-1} B^\top P A, \\ P &= A^\top P A + Q \\ &\quad - A^\top P B (R + B^\top P B)^{-1} B^\top P A, \end{aligned}

the second being the discrete algebraic Riccati equation, the DARE. Its stabilising solution $P \succ 0$ is the matrix of the optimal cost: $J_\infty^\star(x_0) = x_0^\top P x_0$ [[ref:4]]. Chapter [[a14]] derives both formulas from Bellman's recursion; here they are used.

**The canonical numbers.** For $Q = \mathrm{diag}(1, 0.2)$ and $R = 0.2$,

$$K = (0.7396,\ 1.2604), \qquad P = \begin{pmatrix} 1.7042 & 0.5 \\ 0.5 & 0.7021 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \lambda(A_{\mathrm{cl}}) = 0.1849 \pm 0.2743\,i, \qquad \rho(A_{\mathrm{cl}}) = 0.3308 .$$
%narrow \begin{aligned} K &= (0.7396,\ 1.2604), \quad P = \begin{pmatrix} 1.7042 & 0.5 \\ 0.5 & 0.7021 \end{pmatrix}, \\ \lambda(A_{\mathrm{cl}}) &= 0.1849 \pm 0.2743\,i, \quad \rho(A_{\mathrm{cl}}) = 0.3308 . \end{aligned}

The eigenvalues are complex, so the loop spirals in, as the hero's plane shows; the spectral radius a third means the state shrinks by about two thirds per step once the transient is over. The eigenvalues of $P$ are 0.495 and 1.911: positive definite, as a cost must be.

**Sign convention.** The course writes $u = -Kx$, hence $A_{\mathrm{cl}} = A - BK$. Texts that write $u = Fx$ have $F = -K$ and the same loop as $A + BF$. Check before copying a formula: with the canonical gain, $A + BK$ has eigenvalues 0.630 and 3.000, an unstable loop, which is what the wrong sign does (exercise 2).

## The identity: the value drops by exactly the stage cost {#identity}

Under $u = -Kx$ and with $V(x) = x^\top Px$ from the DARE, the Lyapunov decrease is not merely negative. Expanding $(A - BK)^\top P (A - BK)$ and using $B^\top PA = (R + B^\top PB)K$ gives

$$\boxed{A_{\mathrm{cl}}^\top P A_{\mathrm{cl}} - P = -(Q + K^\top R K)}, \qquad\text{so}\qquad \boxed{V(x^+) - V(x) = -\,q(x, -Kx)} \quad \text{for every } x,$$
%narrow \begin{aligned} A_{\mathrm{cl}}^\top P A_{\mathrm{cl}} - P &= -(Q + K^\top R K), \\ V(x^+) - V(x) &= -\,q(x, -Kx). \end{aligned}

where $q(x, u) = x^\top Qx + u^\top Ru$ is the stage cost. The value decreases, at every step, by exactly the price paid at that step, which is positive for every $x \ne 0$ when $Q \succ 0$, as it is here. Exercise 1 proves the identity; the numbers script checks it to $8 \times 10^{-16}$, and on 100 random states to $8 \times 10^{-15}$.

**Read the hero.** From $x_0 = (-3.6, 2.4)$ the value falls $17.490, 3.352, 0.135, 0.0166, 0.0044, \dots$, and the stage costs are $14.138, 3.217, 0.119, 0.0121, \dots$: each difference of consecutive values is the stage cost before it, to round-off. The first move is $u_0 = -Kx_0 = -0.362$, within the bound $|u| \le 1$ from this start, but not from every start: the LQR is an unconstrained law, and $-Kx$ exceeds the bound on most of the box. Chapter [[a10]] computes exactly where it does not, and that region is chapter [[a11]]'s terminal set.

**Why this identity is the heart of the course.** A terminal cost $V_f$ in an MPC problem stands for the cost of everything after the horizon. The theorem of chapter [[a11]] needs $V_f$ to decrease under some local controller by at least the stage cost; the LQR value decreases by exactly it. So the pair $(K, P)$ from the DARE is a terminal controller and terminal cost ready made, and the finite-horizon controller that uses them is, inside the terminal set, the LQR itself.

## Finite horizons converging to P {#recursion}

**The Riccati recursion.** Chapter [[a14]] shows that the optimal cost of a horizon-$N$ problem with terminal weight $P_f$ is quadratic, $J_k^\star(x) = x^\top P_k x$, with $P_N = P_f$ and the backward recursion

$$P_{k} = Q + A^\top P_{k+1} A - A^\top P_{k+1} B\, (R + B^\top P_{k+1} B)^{-1} B^\top P_{k+1} A, \qquad K_k = (R + B^\top P_{k+1} B)^{-1} B^\top P_{k+1} A .$$
%narrow \begin{aligned} P_{k} &= Q + A^\top P_{k+1} A \\ &\quad - A^\top P_{k+1} B\, (R + B^\top P_{k+1} B)^{-1} B^\top P_{k+1} A, \\ K_k &= (R + B^\top P_{k+1} B)^{-1} B^\top P_{k+1} A . \end{aligned}

The DARE is its fixed point. Started from $P_f = P$ it never moves; started from any other $P_f \succeq 0$ it converges to $P$, and the hero's Riccati solver is exactly this recursion run until it stops changing.

**How fast.** Write $\mathrm{Ric}(X)$ for the map above, so that $P_{N-j} = \mathrm{Ric}^j(P_f)$, and let $K_X$, $A_X = A - BK_X$ be the gain and loop computed from $X$. Evaluating the completed square at a non-optimal gain gives the sandwich

$$A_X^\top (X - P) A_X \;\preceq\; \mathrm{Ric}(X) - P \;\preceq\; A_{\mathrm{cl}}^\top (X - P) A_{\mathrm{cl}} .$$

If $P_f \succeq P$, every iterate stays above $P$ and $\lVert \mathrm{Ric}^j(P_f) - P \rVert \le \lVert A_{\mathrm{cl}}^j \rVert^2\, \lVert P_f - P \rVert$: the error decays at the rate $\rho(A_{\mathrm{cl}})^2 = 0.109$ per step. From below the iterates rise towards $P$ at the same asymptotic rate, since $K_X \to K$. The closed loop propagates the cost-to-go error, twice, because the value is quadratic. The numbers script runs the recursion from $P_f \in \{0, Q, 10P\}$: all three reach $P$ to $10^{-12}$ in thirteen or fourteen steps; from $Q$ the error falls from $3.0 \times 10^{-2}$ after two steps to $2.9 \times 10^{-10}$ after ten, a factor 0.10 per step on geometric average against $\rho^2 = 0.109$, with a period-three wobble from the complex eigenvalues; both sandwich inequalities hold to $10^{-15}$.

**What this says about horizons.** A finite-horizon controller with terminal weight $P_f$ applies the gain $K_0$ of the recursion, and $K_0 \to K$ as the horizon grows: a long enough horizon with any terminal weight ends up applying the LQR law. With $P_f = P$ the horizon does not matter at all, which is the unconstrained version of chapter [[a11]]'s statement that the LQR ingredients make the MPC exact inside the terminal set. With $P_f = 0$ and $N = 1$ the recursion gives $K_0 = 0$: the horizon-one pathology of chapter [[a06]], in matrix form.

## The code behind it {#code}

The package solves the DARE with SciPy and derives the gain from it.

::: code advanced_mpc_lmpc/linear.py:dlqr
`solve_discrete_are` returns the stabilising solution $P$ of the DARE; SciPy solves it by a Schur decomposition rather than by iteration, which is faster and exact to round-off for any size. The gain follows from $P$ by one linear solve, $K = (R + B^\top PB)^{-1} B^\top PA$, written as `np.linalg.solve` rather than an inverse. The returned eigenvalues are those of $A - BK$, the course's sign convention, so that `max(abs(eig)) < 1` is the Schur test on the closed loop. Every chapter that needs $K$ or $P$ calls this function.
:::

The browser demos cannot call SciPy, so they run the recursion of [[#recursion|Part 5]] from $P_f = Q$ until it stops changing, in `mpc-lib.js`:

```javascript
function dlqr(A, B, Q, R, iters = 10000) {
  let P = Q;
  for (let it = 0; it < iters; it++) {
    const BtP = mm(tr(B), P), S = add(R, mm(BtP, B)), K = mm(inv(S), mm(BtP, A));
    const Pn = add(Q, sub(mm(tr(A), mm(P, A)), mm(tr(mm(BtP, A)), K)));
    const diff = Math.max(...sub(Pn, P).flat().map(Math.abs)); P = Pn; if (diff < 1e-14) break;
  }
  const BtP = mm(tr(B), P), K = mm(inv(add(R, mm(BtP, B))), mm(BtP, A)), Acl = sub(A, mm(B, K));
  return { K, P, Acl, eig: eig2(Acl) };
}
```

Each pass is one step of the Riccati recursion, $P \leftarrow Q + A^\top PA - (B^\top PA)^\top K$ with $K$ computed from the current $P$; the loop stops when two iterates agree to $10^{-14}$, which for the canonical system takes about twenty-five steps. The two implementations agree to $10^{-14}$ on $K$ and $P$, which is how the pages' numbers are checked against the scripts'.

## Common pitfalls {#pitfalls}

1. **"Stable means $\operatorname{Re}\lambda < 0$."** That is continuous time. For $x_{k+1} = A_{\mathrm{cl}} x_k$ the condition is $|\lambda| < 1$.
2. **"$A - BK$ and $A + BK$ are the same up to convention."** They are two different loops. With the canonical gain one has spectral radius 0.33 and the other an eigenvalue of 3.
3. **"The LQR respects the input bound."** It is an unconstrained law; $-Kx$ exceeds any bound far enough from the origin. Chapter [[a10]] computes where it does not.
4. **"A Lyapunov function is just a decreasing quantity."** It must also be positive definite and vanish only at the target; the quadratic $x^\top Px$ with $P \succ 0$ is the simplest that qualifies, and the Lyapunov equation has no positive-definite solution when the loop is not Schur.

## Check your understanding {#check}

:::: qa
Try to answer each question before opening it.

::: q 1. Why does the discrete-time test look at the modulus of the eigenvalues?
Because $x_k = A^k x_0$ and $A^k$ decays exactly when every eigenvalue satisfies $|\lambda| < 1$. In continuous time the solution is $e^{At}$, which decays when every eigenvalue has negative real part.
:::
::: q 2. What two matrix conditions make $x^\top Px$ a Lyapunov function for $x^+ = A_{\mathrm{cl}} x$?
$P \succ 0$, so that $V$ is positive away from the origin, and $A_{\mathrm{cl}}^\top P A_{\mathrm{cl}} - P \prec 0$, so that $V$ decreases along every nonzero trajectory.
:::
::: q 3. How is $P$ found for a given stable gain?
By solving the discrete Lyapunov equation $A_{\mathrm{cl}}^\top P A_{\mathrm{cl}} - P = -W$ for any $W \succ 0$, a linear system in the entries of $P$ with a unique positive-definite solution when $A_{\mathrm{cl}}$ is Schur.
:::
::: q 4. What is the DARE, and what does its solution mean?
The fixed point of the Riccati recursion, $P = A^\top PA - A^\top PB(R + B^\top PB)^{-1}B^\top PA + Q$. Its stabilising solution is the matrix of the infinite-horizon optimal cost, $J_\infty^\star(x) = x^\top Px$, and the gain $K = (R + B^\top PB)^{-1}B^\top PA$ attains it.
:::
::: q 5. State the identity that makes the LQR value a terminal cost.
$A_{\mathrm{cl}}^\top P A_{\mathrm{cl}} - P = -(Q + K^\top RK)$, equivalently $V(x^+) - V(x) = -q(x, -Kx)$: the value drops by exactly the stage cost, which is the terminal decrease condition of chapter [[a11]] with equality.
:::
::: q 6. At what rate does the Riccati recursion converge, and why?
At $\rho(A_{\mathrm{cl}})^2$ per step asymptotically, 0.109 here: the sandwich inequality shows that the closed loop propagates the error $X - P$, and the value being quadratic squares the factor.
:::
::: q 7. What happens to the LQR move far from the origin?
It grows linearly with the state and exceeds any input bound: from the corner of the box the canonical gain demands more than 1. The LQR is unconstrained, and the terminal set of chapter [[a11]] is exactly the region where it is not.
:::

::::: exercises
:::: exercise Prove the identity
From the DARE, prove $(A - BK)^\top P(A - BK) - P = -(Q + K^\top RK)$.
::: hint
Let $S = R + B^\top PB$, so that $K = S^{-1}B^\top PA$ and $B^\top PA = SK$. Expand the left side and use the DARE in the form $P = A^\top PA - K^\top SK + Q$.
:::
::: solution
Expand: $(A - BK)^\top P (A - BK) = A^\top PA - A^\top PBK - K^\top B^\top PA + K^\top B^\top PBK = A^\top PA - K^\top SK - K^\top SK + K^\top (S - R)K = A^\top PA - K^\top SK - K^\top RK$, using $A^\top PB = (B^\top PA)^\top = K^\top S$ and $B^\top PB = S - R$. The DARE reads $P = A^\top PA - A^\top PB S^{-1} B^\top PA + Q = A^\top PA - K^\top SK + Q$. Subtracting, $(A - BK)^\top P(A - BK) - P = -K^\top RK - Q$. The numbers script's residual is $8 \times 10^{-16}$.

```python
from advanced_mpc_lmpc.linear import dlqr
K, P, eig = dlqr(A, B, Q, R); Acl = A - B @ K
print(np.abs(Acl.T @ P @ Acl - P + Q + K.T @ R @ K).max())   # about 8e-16
```

Fourteen steps from 0, thirteen from $Q$ and from $10P$, with the limit agreeing with `dlqr` to $4 \times 10^{-16}$. The geometric-mean rate from step 2 to step 10 is 0.13, 0.10 and 0.10 against $\rho^2 = 0.109$; the step-by-step ratios wobble with period three because the closed-loop eigenvalues are complex with argument near $56°$, close to a third of a turn.

```python
def ric(P): return Q + A.T @ P @ A - A.T @ P @ B @ np.linalg.solve(R + B.T @ P @ B, B.T @ P @ A)
for Pf in (np.zeros((2, 2)), Q, 10 * P):
    Pk, errs = Pf.copy(), []
    for k in range(30):
        Pk = ric(Pk); errs.append(np.abs(Pk - P).max())
    print(next(k + 1 for k, e in enumerate(errs) if e < 1e-12), (errs[9] / errs[1]) ** (1 / 8))
```

## The chapter on one page {#summary}

::: steps
- **Schur**: discrete-time stability is $\rho(A_{\mathrm{cl}}) < 1$, not negative real parts; the cart's $A$ has both eigenvalues at 1 and coasts.
- **Lyapunov**: $V = x^\top Px$ with $P \succ 0$ and $A_{\mathrm{cl}}^\top PA_{\mathrm{cl}} - P \prec 0$ certifies stability; for any Schur loop the Lyapunov equation supplies $P$.
- **LQR**: $K = (R + B^\top PB)^{-1}B^\top PA$ with $P$ from the DARE; canonical $K = (0.7396, 1.2604)$, $\rho = 0.331$, complex eigenvalues, a spiral.
- **The identity**: $A_{\mathrm{cl}}^\top PA_{\mathrm{cl}} - P = -(Q + K^\top RK)$, so $V$ drops by exactly $q$: $17.49 \to 3.35 \to 0.135$ from $(-3.6, 2.4)$.
- **The recursion**: $P_{N-j} = \mathrm{Ric}^j(P_f) \to P$ at the rate $\rho^2 = 0.109$ per step; thirteen or fourteen steps to $10^{-12}$ from 0, $Q$ or $10P$.
- **The sign**: $u = -Kx$ gives $A - BK$; the other sign gives an eigenvalue of 3.
- **The bound**: the LQR is unconstrained; $-Kx$ exceeds $|u| \le 1$ over most of the box, which is chapter A10's problem and chapter A11's terminal set.
:::

### The vocabulary, in one table

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hurwitz, Schur | Eigenvalues with negative real part; eigenvalues inside the unit circle. |
| Spectral radius $\rho$ | The largest eigenvalue modulus; the asymptotic shrink factor per step. |
| Lyapunov function | Positive definite, zero at the target, decreasing along the loop. |
| Lyapunov equation | $A_{\mathrm{cl}}^\top PA_{\mathrm{cl}} - P = -W$; solvable with $P \succ 0$ exactly when $A_{\mathrm{cl}}$ is Schur. |
| LQR | The feedback minimising $\sum x^\top Qx + u^\top Ru$ over an infinite horizon. |
| DARE | The discrete algebraic Riccati equation; its stabilising solution is the optimal cost matrix. |
| Riccati recursion | The backward iteration whose fixed point is the DARE; the finite-horizon cost matrices. |
| The identity | $V(x^+) - V(x) = -q(x, -Kx)$ under the LQR: the terminal decrease with equality. |

**Scope.** Unconstrained linear systems with quadratic costs and a measured state. Constraints enter in chapter [[a10]], where the LQR's admissible region is computed, and the identity becomes the terminal condition of chapter [[a11]]; the derivation of the Riccati recursion from Bellman's principle is chapter [[a14]]'s.
