## The disturbed plant, and the line of the proof that breaks {#why}

**The plant.** Let the cart be pushed by something the model does not know, a gust, a slope that changes, a motor that delivers a little more or less than asked:

$$x^+ = Ax + Bu + w, \qquad w \in \mathcal W = \{w : |w_1| \le w_{\max},\ |w_2| \le w_{\max}\},$$

with the canonical $A$, $B$ and $w_{\max} = 0.1$. The disturbance is bounded but otherwise arbitrary: it may be random, it may be adversarial, it may be constant. The controller never sees it, only its effect on the next measured state.

**What breaks.** Chapter [[a11]] proved recursive feasibility by the shifted sequence: the plan made at $x_0$ predicts $x_1$, the controller applies $u_0^\star$, and at the next step the shifted plan is feasible *because the measured state is $x_1$*. With a disturbance the measured state is $x_1 + w_0$. The shifted plan starts from a state it was not made for; its predicted states are off by $w_0$, then by $Aw_0 + w_1$, and so on, and nothing in the nominal argument bounds where they end up. The feasibility at the next step becomes a hope.

::: callout warm
**Inherent robustness is not a guarantee.** A nominal MPC re-solved from the measured state often survives small disturbances, because the next problem is usually feasible anyway. The numbers script runs chapter A11's controller on the disturbed cart from every grid state of its region with three disturbance sequences: 512 of 531 runs survive. The 19 that do not all start on the boundary of $\mathcal C_\infty$, where one unlucky push leaves the box, as from $(3, 1.5)$: the first step lands at $p_1 = 4.002$, outside the box by two millimetres, and then no feasible plan exists. Surviving most of the time is an observation; the point of this chapter is a theorem.
:::

## Plan for a copy, feed back the error {#error}

Tube MPC keeps two states. The *nominal state* $z$ evolves under the model with no disturbance and is what the optimiser plans for; the *real state* $x$ is what the plant does. The applied input is the nominal plan's first move plus a linear correction of the gap between the two:

$$\boxed{u = v - K(x - z)}, \qquad z^+ = Az + Bv, \qquad x^+ = Ax + Bu + w,$$

with $K$ the LQR gain of chapter [[a07]], $K = (0.7396, 1.2604)$. Subtracting the two updates, the error $e = x - z$ obeys

$$\boxed{e^+ = (A - BK)\,e + w}:$$

a stable linear system, $\rho(A - BK) = 0.331$, driven by the disturbance alone. Whatever the optimiser does with $v$, the error between plant and plan is governed by $A_K = A - BK$ and $\mathcal W$, nothing else. That decoupling is the whole trick: the optimiser handles the nominal copy, the feedback handles the disturbance, and the two never interfere.

**Why not simply re-solve from $x$.** Re-solving from the measured state, as the nominal controller does, lets the optimiser choose a different plan at every step, and the gap between consecutive plans depends on the disturbance in a way nothing bounds. Fixing the error feedback to a known linear law is what makes the error set computable in advance.

## The minimal robust positively invariant set {#rpi}

Where can the error be? Start at $e_0 = 0$ (the nominal state is initialised at the measured one). After one step $e_1 = w_0 \in \mathcal W$; after two, $e_2 = A_K w_0 + w_1 \in A_K \mathcal W \oplus \mathcal W$; after $s$ steps,

$$e_s \in \mathcal F_s = \mathcal W \oplus A_K \mathcal W \oplus \cdots \oplus A_K^{s-1} \mathcal W,$$

a Minkowski sum of shrinking affine images of the disturbance box, exactly the operations of chapter [[a08]]. Because $A_K$ is Schur, the terms shrink geometrically and the sums converge to the *minimal robust positively invariant set* $\mathcal F_\infty$: the smallest set that the error can never leave once inside.

::: define What is a robust positively invariant set?
**In plain words:** a region of error space that the disturbance cannot push the error out of: start inside, and whatever $w$ the next steps bring, you stay inside. The minimal one is the set of all errors a disturbance sequence can produce from zero.

**More precisely:** $\mathcal E$ is robust positively invariant for $e^+ = A_K e + w$ if $A_K \mathcal E \oplus \mathcal W \subseteq \mathcal E$. The minimal one is $\mathcal F_\infty = \bigoplus_{k \ge 0} A_K^k \mathcal W$, a limit that is not a polytope in general; a polytopic outer approximation is $\mathcal E = (1 - \alpha)^{-1} \mathcal F_s$ for any $s$ and $\alpha \in [0, 1)$ with $A_K^s \mathcal W \subseteq \alpha \mathcal W$ (Raković, Kerrigan, Kouramas and Mayne [[ref:2]]).

- Minkowski sum $\mathcal A \oplus \mathcal B$ :: All sums of a point of each; chapter [[a08]] computes it from the vertices.
- Partial sum $\mathcal F_s$ :: Where the error can be after $s$ steps from zero; $s$ more vertices each step.
- Scaling $\alpha$ :: How much $s$ steps of the closed loop shrink the disturbance box; the tail beyond $s$ is at most $\alpha / (1 - \alpha)$ of the head.
:::

**The numbers.** With $w_{\max} = 0.1$ the partial sums have 4, 8, 12, 16 vertices and areas 0.040, 0.124, 0.163, 0.172 for $s = 1$ to 4; by $s = 8$ the area is 0.1768 with 32 vertices, and it does not change to four decimals afterwards. The scaling at $s = 8$ is tiny, $\alpha = 6.1 \times 10^{-4}$, since $\rho(A_K)^8 \approx 1.4 \times 10^{-4}$, so the outer approximation $\mathcal E = \mathcal F_8 / (1 - \alpha)$ has area 0.1770 and extends 0.234 in position and 0.260 in velocity. The numbers script checks the defining inclusion directly: every vertex of $A_K \mathcal E \oplus \mathcal W$ lies in $\mathcal E$, with the largest support-function gap $10^{-10}$. The hero draws $\mathcal E$ translated to every nominal state: that is the tube.

## Constraints shrunk by the error set {#tighten}

If the error is always in $\mathcal E$, then keeping the real state in $\mathcal X$ is the same as keeping the nominal state in $\mathcal X$ with a margin of $\mathcal E$ removed in every direction, the *Pontryagin difference*

$$\bar{\mathcal X} = \mathcal X \ominus \mathcal E = \{z : z + e \in \mathcal X \text{ for all } e \in \mathcal E\}.$$

For a polytope $\{x : H_x x \le h_x\}$ this is row by row: each bound $h_i$ loses the support function of $\mathcal E$ in the direction $H_i$, the farthest an error can reach that way,

$$\boxed{\bar h_i = h_i - \max_{e \in \mathcal E} H_i e}.$$

The input bound shrinks the same way, since the applied input is $v - Ke$ and $|Ke|$ can reach $\max_{e \in \mathcal E} |Ke| = 0.306$:

$$\bar{\mathcal U} = \mathcal U \ominus K\mathcal E = \{v : |v| \le 1 - 0.306 = 0.694\}.$$

The box $|p| \le 4$, $|v| \le 3$ becomes $|p| \le 3.766$, $|v| \le 2.740$. Two things are worth noticing. The velocity bound loses more than the position bound, because the error set is taller than it is wide: the disturbance enters the velocity directly and the position only through it. And the input loses almost a third of its range, which is what the tube feedback needs to hold the error in: the price of robustness is paid mostly by the actuator.

## The tube MPC law and its theorem {#law}

Put the pieces together. At each step, with the measured state $x$ and the nominal state $z$:

1. solve chapter [[a11]]'s problem for the nominal state, with the tightened sets, the horizon $N$, the terminal set $\bar{\mathcal X}_f$ computed inside $\bar{\mathcal X}$ with the tightened input bound, and the Riccati terminal cost;
2. apply $u = v_0^\star - K(x - z)$ to the plant;
3. advance the nominal state, $z^+ = Az + Bv_0^\star$, and repeat.

The nominal problem is exactly the one of chapter [[a11]] on a smaller box with a weaker motor, so its terminal ingredients are computed the same way: $\bar{\mathcal X}_K$ where the LQR input stays within $\pm 0.694$, its maximal invariant subset $\bar{\mathcal X}_f$ (4 vertices, area 3.52, two iterations), and the five-step feasible region (area 29.49) against a $\bar{\mathcal C}_\infty$ of area 31.55.

::: callout
**The theorem (Mayne, Seron and Raković [[ref:1]]).** If $\mathcal E$ is robust positively invariant for $e^+ = A_K e + w$, $z_0 = x_0$, and the nominal problem on $\bar{\mathcal X}$, $\bar{\mathcal U}$ with terminal ingredients is feasible at $z_0$, then for every disturbance sequence in $\mathcal W$: the nominal problem is feasible at every step (chapter A11's proof, now true because $z$ really does move as predicted); $x_t \in z_t \oplus \mathcal E$ for all $t$, hence $x_t \in \mathcal X$ and $u_t \in \mathcal U$; and $z_t \to 0$, hence $x_t$ converges to $\mathcal E$.
:::

The proof has no new ideas. The nominal state is undisturbed, so the shifted sequence is feasible and the Lyapunov argument of chapter [[a11]] holds for $z$. The error starts at zero and $\mathcal E$ is robust invariant, so $e_t \in \mathcal E$ by induction. Then $x_t = z_t + e_t \in \bar{\mathcal X} \oplus \mathcal E \subseteq \mathcal X$ and $u_t = v_t - Ke_t \in \bar{\mathcal U} \oplus (-K\mathcal E) \subseteq \mathcal U$, which is what the tightening was designed for. The guarantee is for every $w$ in the box, not on average and not with high probability.

## Forty disturbed steps, with and without the tube {#experiment}

**The tube controller.** From $x_0 = (-3, 2)$, inside the tube region, with $w_{\max} = 0.1$ and five different disturbance sequences, the numbers script reports: zero box violations in every run, the largest applied input 0.81 (below the bound of 1, as the theorem promises, with the nominal part never above 0.694 and the correction $Ke$ at most 0.306), every error inside $\mathcal E$ at every step, with the largest errors 0.15 in position and 0.18 in velocity. The nominal state reaches the origin, $\lVert z_t \rVert = 3.61, 0.75, 0.14, 0.005$ at $t = 0, 2, 4, 6$; the real state cannot, and ends after forty steps at a norm between 0.09 and 0.23, inside $\mathcal E$, jittering with the disturbance. The first applied input is $-0.591$, the nominal plan's own first move, since $e_0 = 0$.

**The nominal controller on the same plant.** Chapter A11's controller, re-solved from the measured state with the full box and the full input, survives the same five sequences from $(-3, 2)$, saturating at $|u| = 1$ where the tube controller stayed at 0.81. It survives most starts too: the grid search of Part 1 finds 19 failures in 531 runs, all from the boundary of $\mathcal C_\infty$. The tube controller's region does not contain any of those 19 starts: it says no at once where the nominal controller says yes and then fails. That is the shape of the trade: the tube controller is honest about where it can keep its promise, at the cost of a region a quarter smaller.

Switch the hero to the nominal controller and move the start to $(3, 1.5)$ to see one of the failures, with the seed set to 1.

## The price, and where tube MPC sits {#price}

| $w_{\max}$ | area of $\mathcal E$ | tightened box | nominal input | $\bar{\mathcal X}_f$ area | $\bar{\mathcal C}_\infty$ area | region at $N = 5$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (chapter A11) | 0 | $4 \times 3$ | 1 | 7.31 | 39.25 | 39.25 |
| 0.05 | 0.044 | $3.88 \times 2.87$ | 0.847 | 5.25 | 35.47 | 35.11 |
| 0.1 | 0.177 | $3.77 \times 2.74$ | 0.694 | 3.52 | 31.55 | 29.49 |
| 0.15 | 0.398 | $3.65 \times 2.61$ | 0.541 | 2.14 | 27.26 | 22.81 |

Three costs appear. The region shrinks, by a quarter at $w_{\max} = 0.1$ and by 42 percent at 0.15, so starts the nominal controller would accept are refused. Convergence is to the set $\mathcal E$ and not to the origin: the real state keeps moving as long as the disturbance does. And the tube feedback uses actuator range that the optimiser can no longer plan with, so the nominal plan is slower than the unconstrained one would be.

**Where it sits.** This is the rigid-tube MPC of Mayne, Seron and Raković [[ref:1]], the construction Rawlings, Mayne and Diehl [[ref:3]] present as the reference robust design. It needs a linear model, a known bound on the disturbance, and the sets of chapters [[a08]] to [[a10]], which is why it fits this course. Beyond it: homothetic and elastic tubes let the cross-section change along the horizon; min-max MPC optimises over feedback policies instead of fixing $K$; constraint tightening without an explicit $\mathcal E$ trades the invariant set for a sequence of margins; and stochastic MPC replaces the worst case by chance constraints when the disturbance has a known distribution and an occasional violation is acceptable. For a nonlinear plant the error dynamics are no longer linear and $\mathcal E$ must be built differently, which is where chapter [[c03]]'s linearisations and chapter [[b04]]'s data-driven models leave the guarantees of this page behind.

## The code behind it {#code}

The error set is built from two functions of chapter A8's toolkit. The first maps a polytope through a matrix by mapping its vertices.

::: code advanced_mpc_lmpc/polytopes.py:affine_image_2d
The function is honest about its scope: it works in two dimensions, through the vertices, and rebuilds the half-space form from the hull of the images. For $A_K^k \mathcal W$ the matrix shrinks and rotates the disturbance box, so each image is a smaller, tilted parallelogram, and `from_vertices_2d` recovers its four half-spaces.
:::

::: code advanced_mpc_lmpc/polytopes.py:minkowski_sum_2d
All pairwise sums of the two vertex sets, then the hull. With four vertices each, the sum of two parallelograms has at most eight vertices; the numbers script's $\mathcal F_s$ gains four vertices per term until the images are too small to add corners, which is why $\mathcal F_8$ has 32 and $\mathcal F_{12}$ only 46. The pairwise construction is quadratic in the vertex counts, acceptable here and the reason higher dimensions use other representations.
:::

The tube loop itself is six lines, written in the numbers script and reproduced by the hero:

```python
def tube_loop(mpc, x0, wmax, steps=40, seed=0, K=K, E=E):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    x = np.array(x0, float); z = x.copy()
    for t in range(steps):
        sol = mpc.solve(z)                                   # the nominal problem, from the nominal state
        v = float(sol.U[0].item())
        u = v - float((K @ (x - z)).item())                  # the tube feedback keeps x near z
        w = rng.uniform(-wmax, wmax, 2)
        x = A @ x + B.ravel() * u + w                        # the plant, disturbed
        z = A @ z + B.ravel() * v                            # the nominal model, undisturbed
```

Two lines matter. The problem is solved from `z`, never from `x`: the optimiser is not told the disturbance happened. And `z` is advanced with the model, so the nominal trajectory is exactly a trajectory of chapter A11's controller on a smaller box, with every theorem of that chapter attached.

## Common pitfalls {#pitfalls}

1. **"Re-solving from the measured state is robust enough."** Often, not always: 19 of 531 grid runs failed, and the failures are not announced in advance. The tube gives a region where the promise holds for every disturbance.
2. **"The tube feedback and the LQR of A7 are different things."** The same $K$ serves both; what differs is what it acts on. The LQR regulates the state, the tube feedback regulates the error between plant and plan.
3. **"$\mathcal E$ is where the state ends up."** $\mathcal E$ is where the *error* lives. The real state converges to $z \oplus \mathcal E$ with $z \to 0$, so to $\mathcal E$ around the origin, and only because the nominal state converges.
4. **"A larger $\mathcal E$ is safer."** It is more conservative, not safer. Any robust positively invariant superset works for the proof, but every extra bit of $\mathcal E$ shrinks the constraints and the region. The minimal one, approximated by $\mathcal F_8$, is the right choice.
5. **"The disturbance must be random."** Nothing in the proof uses randomness. The hero draws it uniformly for illustration; the exercise with the worst-case corners holds just as well.

## Check your understanding {#check}

:::: qa
Try to answer each question before opening it.

::: q 1. Which line of chapter A11's recursive-feasibility proof fails under a disturbance?
The one that says the next measured state is the predicted $x_1$. With a disturbance it is $x_1 + w_0$, the shifted plan starts from a state it was not made for, and nothing bounds how far its predictions drift.
:::
::: q 2. Why does the error obey a linear system driven by $w$ alone?
Subtract the nominal update $z^+ = Az + Bv$ from the plant update with $u = v - K(x - z)$: $e^+ = A e - BK e + w = (A - BK) e + w$. The optimiser's choice $v$ cancels out.
:::
::: q 3. What is $\mathcal F_s$ and why does it converge?
The Minkowski sum of $\mathcal W, A_K \mathcal W, \dots, A_K^{s-1} \mathcal W$: where $s$ steps of disturbance can send an error that started at zero. The terms shrink geometrically because $\rho(A_K) < 1$, so the sum converges.
:::
::: q 4. Why is $\mathcal F_8 / (1 - \alpha)$ robust positively invariant?
Because $A_K^8 \mathcal W \subseteq \alpha \mathcal W$ bounds the neglected tail of the infinite sum by a fraction $\alpha / (1 - \alpha)$ of $\mathcal F_8$ itself; scaling by $(1 - \alpha)^{-1}$ covers it. The numbers script checks the inclusion $A_K \mathcal E \oplus \mathcal W \subseteq \mathcal E$ vertex by vertex.
:::
::: q 5. How is a box tightened by a polytope?
Row by row: each bound loses the support function of $\mathcal E$ in that row's direction, the farthest an error can reach that way. The position bound loses 0.234, the velocity bound 0.260, the input bound $\max |Ke| = 0.306$.
:::
::: q 6. What does the theorem promise, and for which disturbances?
Feasibility of the nominal problem at every step, $x_t \in z_t \oplus \mathcal E$, hence all constraints satisfied, and convergence of $x$ to $\mathcal E$: for every disturbance sequence inside $\mathcal W$, adversarial ones included.
:::
::: q 7. Why does the applied input never reach 1 although the plant allows it?
The nominal plan uses at most 0.694 and the correction at most 0.306; they add to at most 1, and only when both saturate in the same direction, which the runs did not do (0.81 at most).
:::
::: q 8. What is lost?
A quarter of the region at $w_{\max} = 0.1$, convergence to a set instead of a point, and a third of the actuator range reserved for the error feedback.
:::

:::: exercise The error recursion by hand
Starting from $x^+ = Ax + Bu + w$, $z^+ = Az + Bv$ and $u = v - K(x - z)$, derive $e^+ = (A - BK)e + w$ and say which assumption about the model it uses.
::: hint
Subtract the two updates and substitute the input.
:::
::: solution
$x^+ - z^+ = A(x - z) + B(u - v) + w = A e - BK e + w$. It uses that the plant and the model share $A$ and $B$ exactly: the disturbance is additive and bounded, but the dynamics are known. A mismatch in $A$ would add a term $\Delta A\, x$ that is not bounded by $\mathcal W$, which is why parametric uncertainty needs a different construction.
:::

::: solution
$\mathcal E$ has area 0.708 and the input bound shrinks to $1 - 0.612 = 0.388$; the tightened box is about $3.53 \times 2.48$. The five-step region shrinks to about 16, and $(-3, 2)$ is outside it: the nominal problem is infeasible at the start. Half the actuator range is now reserved for the tube feedback, and a cart that must be able to brake from $v = 2$ cannot do it with 0.388 in five steps. Either accept a smaller region, lengthen the horizon, or reduce the disturbance with a better model.
:::

## The chapter on one page {#summary}

::: steps
- **The plant** $x^+ = Ax + Bu + w$ with $w$ in a box; the nominal proofs break at "the next state is the predicted one".
- **The tube feedback** $u = v - K(x - z)$ makes the error obey $e^+ = A_K e + w$, independent of the plan.
- **The error set** $\mathcal E \approx \mathcal F_8 / (1 - \alpha)$, Minkowski sums of $A_K^k \mathcal W$: area 0.177, 32 vertices, $\alpha = 6 \times 10^{-4}$.
- **Tightening** row by row by support functions: $3.77 \times 2.74$ and $|v| \le 0.694$.
- **The law**: chapter A11's controller for $z$ on the tightened sets, then $u = v - Ke$; the theorem gives feasibility, $x \in z \oplus \mathcal E$ and convergence to $\mathcal E$ for every disturbance.
- **The experiment**: forty steps from $(-3, 2)$, no violation, $|u| \le 0.81$, $x$ ends inside $\mathcal E$; the nominal controller fails from 19 of 531 boundary runs, none of which the tube controller accepts.
- **The price**: a quarter of the region, convergence to a set, a third of the actuator.
:::

### The vocabulary, in one table

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Nominal state $z$ | The undisturbed copy of the plant that the optimiser plans for. |
| Tube feedback | $u = v - K(x - z)$: the plan's move plus a correction of the error. |
| Error $e = x - z$ | Obeys $e^+ = A_K e + w$, driven by the disturbance alone. |
| Robust positively invariant | $A_K \mathcal E \oplus \mathcal W \subseteq \mathcal E$: the disturbance cannot push the error out. |
| Minimal RPI set, $\mathcal F_s$ | The limit of the partial Minkowski sums; $\mathcal F_s$ the sum of the first $s$ terms. |
| Scaling $\alpha$ | $A_K^s \mathcal W \subseteq \alpha \mathcal W$; $\mathcal F_s / (1 - \alpha)$ is an outer approximation. |
| Pontryagin difference $\mathcal X \ominus \mathcal E$ | The states that stay in $\mathcal X$ under every error in $\mathcal E$; row by row, bounds minus support functions. |
| Tube | The sets $z_t \oplus \mathcal E$ along the nominal trajectory, which contain the real state. |

**Scope.** Rigid-tube MPC for a linear model with an additive disturbance in a known box, computed exactly with the course's two-dimensional polytopes. Parametric uncertainty, nonlinear plants, disturbances without a known bound and output feedback are outside it; the last part says where they lead.
