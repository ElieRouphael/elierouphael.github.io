## General terminal sets: three conditions, one shifted sequence {#general}

Chapter [[a06]] wrote the problem solved at each step with a terminal cost $V_f$ and a terminal constraint $x_N \in \mathcal X_f$, and showed what goes wrong when they are chosen carelessly. Here is what they must satisfy.

::: define What are terminal ingredients?
**In plain words:** a region where the plan must end and a price put on ending there, chosen so that a known local controller could always take over from that region, safely and at a cost the price already accounts for.

**More precisely:** a terminal set $\mathcal X_f \subseteq \mathcal X$, a local law $\kappa_f(x)$ and a terminal cost $V_f$ such that, for every $x \in \mathcal X_f$:

1. positive-definite stage cost: $q(x, u) > 0$ away from the origin, $q(0, 0) = 0$; quantitatively $q(x, u) \ge \alpha_1(\lVert x \rVert)$, which for $x^\top Qx + u^\top Ru$ with $Q \succ 0$ holds with $\alpha_1(s) = \lambda_{\min}(Q)\, s^2$;
2. invariance and admissibility: $\kappa_f(x) \in \mathcal U$ and $Ax + B\kappa_f(x) \in \mathcal X_f$;
3. terminal decrease: $V_f(Ax + B\kappa_f(x)) - V_f(x) \le -q(x, \kappa_f(x))$.

These three assumptions are the conceptual heart of stabilising MPC [[ref:3]]. They mimic the missing infinite-horizon tail: the set says the tail can be continued admissibly, the cost says what the tail costs.

- Shifted sequence :: Yesterday's plan minus its first move, plus one move of the local law: always a feasible candidate.
- Recursive feasibility :: Feasible now implies feasible after the applied move; condition 2 supplies it.
- Lyapunov decrease :: $J_N^\star(x^+) - J_N^\star(x) \le -q(x, u_0^\star)$; condition 3 supplies it.
- Region of attraction :: The feasible set $\mathcal X_N$, certified by the theorem.
:::

**Recursive feasibility.** Suppose at state $x_0$ the optimal sequence is $U^\star = (u_0^\star, \dots, u_{N-1}^\star)$ with predicted states $x_1, \dots, x_N$ and $x_N \in \mathcal X_f$. MPC applies $u_0^\star$ and the system moves to $x_1$. At $x_1$ consider the candidate

$$\boxed{\widetilde U = \big( u_1^\star, \dots, u_{N-1}^\star,\ \kappa_f(x_N) \big)}.$$

It is feasible: its first $N - 1$ states are $x_2, \dots, x_N$, already admissible in the old plan; its last input $\kappa_f(x_N)$ is admissible by condition 2; and its terminal state $x_{N+1} = Ax_N + B\kappa_f(x_N)$ lies in $\mathcal X_f$ by the same condition. Hence

$$\boxed{x_0 \in \mathcal X_N \ \Longrightarrow\ x_1 \in \mathcal X_N}$$

under the nominal model. This is a proof, not a numerical observation, and it uses neither optimality nor condition 3: any feasible plan has a feasible successor.

**The value decreases.** The optimiser at $x_1$ can only do better than the feasible candidate, so

$$J_N^\star(x_1) \le \sum_{i=1}^{N-1} q(x_i, u_i^\star) + q(x_N, \kappa_f(x_N)) + V_f(x_{N+1}),$$

and subtracting $J_N^\star(x_0) = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} q(x_i, u_i^\star) + V_f(x_N)$,

$$J_N^\star(x_1) - J_N^\star(x_0) \le -q(x_0, u_0^\star) + \underbrace{q(x_N, \kappa_f(x_N)) + V_f(x_{N+1}) - V_f(x_N)}_{\le 0 \text{ by condition 3}} ,$$
%narrow \begin{aligned} &J_N^\star(x_1) - J_N^\star(x_0) \le -q(x_0, u_0^\star) \\ &\quad + \underbrace{q(x_N, \kappa_f(x_N)) + V_f(x_{N+1}) - V_f(x_N)}_{\le 0} \end{aligned}

$$\text{hence}\qquad \boxed{J_N^\star(x_1) - J_N^\star(x_0) \le -q(x_0, u_0^\star)}.$$

The two ingredients have different jobs: the terminal set gives a feasible tail, the terminal cost gives a cost bound on that tail.

$$\boxed{\text{terminal set} \Rightarrow \text{recursive-feasibility mechanism}}, \qquad \boxed{\text{terminal cost} \Rightarrow \text{Lyapunov-decrease mechanism}}.$$
%narrow \begin{aligned} \text{terminal set} &\Rightarrow \text{recursive feasibility}, \\ \text{terminal cost} &\Rightarrow \text{Lyapunov decrease}. \end{aligned}

## From the decrease to asymptotic stability {#lyapunov}

Decrease alone does not prove asymptotic stability; $J_N^\star$ must also be bounded below and above by functions of $\lVert x \rVert$, as chapter [[a07]] required of any Lyapunov function. A function $\alpha : [0, \infty) \to [0, \infty)$ is of class $\mathcal K$ if it is continuous, strictly increasing and $\alpha(0) = 0$. Suppose there are class-$\mathcal K$ functions $\alpha_1, \alpha_2$ and a radius $r > 0$ with:

**Lower bound.** $q(x, u) \ge \alpha_1(\lVert x \rVert)$ for all admissible pairs; since $J_N^\star(x) \ge q(x, u_0^\star)$, this gives $J_N^\star(x) \ge \alpha_1(\lVert x \rVert)$ on $\mathcal X_N$. Condition 1 supplies it.

**Upper bound near the origin.** $J_N^\star(x) \le \alpha_2(\lVert x \rVert)$ for $x \in \mathcal X_N$ with $\lVert x \rVert \le r$; since $J_N^\star \ge 0$ and $J_N^\star(0) = 0$, this is continuity of $J_N^\star$ at the origin.

**Decrease.** $J_N^\star(x^+) - J_N^\star(x) \le -q(x, \kappa_N(x)) \le -\alpha_1(\lVert x \rVert)$ on $\mathcal X_N$: the boxed inequality.

Then the origin is asymptotically stable with region of attraction $\mathcal X_N$, which is positively invariant by the feasibility argument [[ref:2]]. The conditions do different jobs. Summing the decrease along the loop gives $\sum_{t \ge 0} \alpha_1(\lVert x(t) \rVert) \le J_N^\star(x(0)) < \infty$, so $x(t) \to 0$ from every feasible start. The upper bound adds Lyapunov stability, small starts staying small: for $\lVert x(0) \rVert \le r$, $\alpha_1(\lVert x(t) \rVert) \le J_N^\star(x(t)) \le J_N^\star(x(0)) \le \alpha_2(\lVert x(0) \rVert)$, and the right-hand side vanishes as $x(0) \to 0$.

**A terminal set gives the upper bound for free.** With $\mathcal X_f$ containing the origin in its interior, applying $\kappa_f$ for $N$ steps from any $x \in \mathcal X_f$ is a feasible plan by condition 2, and summing condition 3 along it bounds its cost by $V_f(x)$:

$$J_N^\star(x) \le V_f(x) \qquad \text{for all } x \in \mathcal X_f .$$

Any terminal cost with $V_f(x) \le \alpha_2(\lVert x \rVert)$, such as $x^\top Px$ with $\alpha_2(s) = \lambda_{\max}(P)\, s^2$, gives the upper bound on a ball around the origin with no extra assumption. If moreover $\alpha_1(s) = c_1 s^2$ and $J_N^\star(x) \le c_2 \lVert x \rVert^2$ on $\mathcal X_N$, the origin is exponentially stable there:

$$J_N^\star(x(t+1)) \le (1 - c_1/c_2)\, J_N^\star(x(t)), \qquad \lVert x(t) \rVert \le \sqrt{c_2/c_1}\,(1 - c_1/c_2)^{t/2} \lVert x(0) \rVert .$$
%narrow \begin{aligned} J_N^\star(x(t+1)) &\le (1 - c_1/c_2)\, J_N^\star(x(t)), \\ \lVert x(t) \rVert &\le \sqrt{c_2/c_1}\,(1 - c_1/c_2)^{t/2} \lVert x(0) \rVert . \end{aligned}

In the linear-quadratic design $q \ge \lambda_{\min}(Q) \lVert x \rVert^2$ and $V_f \le \lambda_{\max}(P) \lVert x \rVert^2$, so the stability is exponential on the bounded set $\mathcal X_N$, with $\lambda_{\min}(Q) = 0.2$ and $\lambda_{\max}(P) = 1.911$ for the canonical weights.

## The zero terminal constraint: the special case that needs more {#zero}

The simplest terminal set is the origin itself, $\mathcal X_f = \{0\}$, with $\kappa_f = 0$ and $V_f = 0$ [[ref:4]]:

$$J_N^\star(x) = \min_U \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} q(x_k, u_k) \quad \text{s.t.}\quad x_{k+1} = Ax_k + Bu_k, \quad x_k \in \mathcal X, \quad u_k \in \mathcal U, \quad \boxed{x_N = 0}.$$
%narrow \begin{aligned} J_N^\star(x) = \min_U\ &\textstyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} q(x_k, u_k) \\ \text{s.t. } &x_{k+1} = Ax_k + Bu_k,\ x_k \in \mathcal X, \\ &u_k \in \mathcal U,\ \boxed{x_N = 0}. \end{aligned}

Conditions 2 and 3 hold trivially: the equilibrium satisfies $A0 + B0 = 0$, zero is an admissible input, and $V_f = 0$ decreases by $q(0, 0) = 0$. The shifted sequence is $(u_1^\star, \dots, u_{N-1}^\star, 0)$ and the proof of [[#general|Part 1]] goes through unchanged: recursive feasibility and $J_N^\star(x_1) - J_N^\star(x_0) \le -q(x_0, u_0^\star)$.

**What is missing is the upper bound.** With $x_N = 0$ the only admissible plans reach the origin exactly in $N$ steps, and for some systems, typically nonlinear ones, that forces large inputs and a large cost even from tiny states: $J_N^\star$ is then discontinuous at the origin. The extra assumption is local controllability with small inputs: every $x$ with $\lVert x \rVert \le r$ can be steered to the origin in $N$ admissible steps with $\lVert u_k \rVert \le \gamma(\lVert x \rVert)$ for a class-$\mathcal K$ function $\gamma$; that plan's cost then bounds $J_N^\star$. For the linear model it holds when $(A, B)$ is controllable, $N \ge n$ and the origin is interior to $\mathcal X$ and $\mathcal U$: the minimum-norm plan $U = -\mathcal C_N^{\dagger} A^N x$, with the controllability matrix $\mathcal C_N = (A^{N-1}B, \dots, AB, B)$, reaches zero, is linear in $x$, hence admissible for small $x$, and with a quadratic stage cost gives $J_N^\star(x) \le c \lVert x \rVert^2$ near the origin. For the canonical cart and $N = 5$, the state $(0.1, 0.1)$ needs inputs no larger than 0.09 and $(0.5, -0.3)$ no larger than 0.11.

**And what is lost is reach.** Reaching exactly zero within $N$ steps is a strong demand, and [[#regions|Part 5]] measures how much smaller the certified region is. The general terminal set is preferred for both reasons: the upper bound comes free, and the horizon only has to reach a region rather than a point.

## The linear-quadratic ingredients {#lq}

**The terminal cost.** With the quadratic stage cost $q(x, u) = x^\top Qx + u^\top Ru$, $Q \succ 0$, $R \succ 0$, take $\kappa_f(x) = -Kx$ and $V_f(x) = x^\top Px$ with $(K, P)$ from the DARE of chapter [[a07]]. The identity

$$\boxed{(A - BK)^\top P (A - BK) - P = -(Q + K^\top RK)}, \qquad \text{so}\qquad V_f(x^+) - V_f(x) = -q(x, -Kx),$$
%narrow \begin{aligned} (A - BK)^\top P (A - BK) - P &= -(Q + K^\top RK), \\ V_f(x^+) - V_f(x) &= -q(x, -Kx), \end{aligned}

is condition 3 with equality, for every $x$, with no set involved; the numbers script's residual is $8 \times 10^{-16}$. For the canonical weights, $K = (0.7396, 1.2604)$ and $\rho(A - BK) = 0.3308$.

**The terminal set.** Condition 2 is where the constraints enter: the LQR law does not respect the input bound by itself. Require $-Kx \in \mathcal U$, define

$$\mathcal X_K = \{x \in \mathcal X : u_{\min} \le -Kx \le u_{\max}\},$$

and take $\mathcal X_f$ to be the maximal positively invariant set of $x^+ = (A - BK)x$ inside $\mathcal X_K$, computed by chapter [[a10]]'s fixed-point iteration $\Omega_{j+1} = \mathcal X_K \cap A_{\mathrm{cl}}^{-1}(\Omega_j)$ [[ref:5]]. Inside it the LQR input is admissible now, the next state is inside again, and the value decreases by the identity: the three conditions. With the weaker motor $|u| \le 0.5$, $\mathcal X_K$ is a parallelogram of area 6.35 and the iteration stops after two steps with $\mathcal X_f$ of four vertices, $(\pm 1.202, \mp 1.102)$ and $(\pm 1.102, \mp 0.25)$, area 1.83. With the full motor the vertices are $(\pm 2.404, \mp 2.204)$ and $(\pm 2.204, \mp 0.5)$, area 7.31, the set of chapter [[a10]].

**Verifying the conditions numerically.** For a convex polytope and linear maps it suffices to check the vertices: condition 2 holds on the whole set if it holds at the vertices, since the set is convex and $x \mapsto -Kx$ and $x \mapsto A_{\mathrm{cl}}x$ are linear, and condition 3 is an identity in $x$. At each vertex of the weaker-motor $\mathcal X_f$ the input is exactly $\pm 0.5$, on the bound, the successor lies inside, and the decrease residual is below $5 \times 10^{-16}$. The widget does the same check for any $R$ and either motor.

## Zero point against invariant region, and the horizon as a lever {#regions}

**The feasible set is a controllable set.** The states from which the horizon-$N$ problem is feasible are those that can reach $\mathcal X_f$ in $N$ admissible steps: chapter [[a09]]'s $N$-step controllable set, $\mathcal X_N = \mathcal K_N(\mathcal X_f)$, with the singleton $\{0\}$ as target for the zero design. Because $\mathcal X_f$ is control invariant the sets are nested, $\mathcal X_f = \mathcal K_0 \subseteq \mathcal K_1 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq \mathcal C_\infty$, and each is control invariant itself, which is recursive feasibility at the level of sets.

**Same horizon, different reach.** With the weaker motor and $N = 5$ the numbers script finds areas of 15.10 for design A, $x_N = 0$, and 23.31 for design B, $x_N \in \mathcal X_f$, against 30.29 for the maximal control-invariant set $\mathcal C_\infty$: 50 and 77 percent of everything that could ever be certified. Design B lets the optimiser stop at any state of the certified region because the local LQR law can safely take over from there; design A forces the five online steps to do all the work of reaching the equilibrium exactly. The state $(-3.5, -0.5)$ exposes the difference: it is infeasible for the exact-zero requirement and feasible with $x_N \in \mathcal X_f$, and the hero starts there.

**The horizon is a second lever.** As $N$ grows both regions grow towards $\mathcal C_\infty$: for the zero design the areas at $N = 1, \dots, 12$ are $0, 1.0, 4.0, 10.0, 15.1, 18.97, 22.42, 25.42, 27.75, 29.38, 30.21, 30.29$, and for the set design at $N = 0, \dots, 10$ they are $1.83, 5.33, 11.76, 16.31, 20.08, 23.31, 26.0, 28.15, 29.6, 30.26, 30.29$. The zero design fills $\mathcal C_\infty$ at $N = 12$, the set design at $N = 10$. A longer horizon sees farther and enlarges the region at more online cost: more decision variables and constraints at every sample. Terminal ingredients encode reliable information about the tail instead of forcing the horizon to optimise it explicitly.

**Why the weaker motor.** With the full motor the same areas are $4.0, 15.6, 24.57, 31.75, 37.0, 39.25$ for the zero design at $N = 2, \dots, 7$ and $19.35, 27.62, 34.34, 38.07, 39.25$ for the set design at $N = 1, \dots, 5$: both fill $\mathcal C_\infty$, of area 39.25, within seven steps, and at $N = 5$ the set design already certifies everything. ## The closed loop, checked {#loop}

The controller solves at every step

$$\min\ x_N^\top Px_N + \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} (x_k^\top Qx_k + u_k^\top Ru_k) \quad \text{s.t.}\quad x_{k+1} = Ax_k + Bu_k, \quad x_k \in \mathcal X, \quad u_k \in \mathcal U, \quad x_N \in \mathcal X_f,$$
%narrow \begin{aligned} \min\ &x_N^\top Px_N + \textstyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} (x_k^\top Qx_k + u_k^\top Ru_k) \\ \text{s.t. } &x_{k+1} = Ax_k + Bu_k,\ x_k \in \mathcal X, \\ &u_k \in \mathcal U,\ x_N \in \mathcal X_f, \end{aligned}

with $N = 5$ and the weaker motor, applies the first input and re-solves. From $x_0 = (-3.5, -0.5)$ the first problem succeeds with first input $+0.5$, saturated, and predicted terminal state $(-0.614, 0.757)$, inside $\mathcal X_f$. The loop reaches $\lVert x \rVert < 10^{-3}$ in 12 steps with the input saturated on five of them.

**Use the full theoretical cost.** The QP solver drops terms that are constant in the decision vector, which is legitimate for optimisation and wrong for a Lyapunov check. For diagnostics, reconstruct

$$J_N^\star(x) = x_0^\top Qx_0 + \sum_{k=1}^{N-1} x_k^\top Qx_k + \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} u_k^\top Ru_k + x_N^\top Px_N,$$

the quantity in the proof. Along the loop it falls $50.164, 37.716, 23.604, 11.254, \dots$; the first drop, 12.448, exceeds the first stage cost 12.35 by the tail term the proof bounds by zero.

**The Lyapunov inequality, checked.** The theorem predicts $J_N^\star(x(t+1)) - J_N^\star(x(t)) + q(x(t), u(t)) \le 0$. Along the loop the residual is at most $1.4 \times 10^{-14}$, round-off, and positive on none of the eleven steps. Why it is nearly an equality: the appended terminal controller is the infinite-horizon LQR and the Riccati cost satisfies $V_f(x^+) - V_f(x) = -q(x, -Kx)$ exactly, so the bracketed tail term is zero once the plan ends inside $\mathcal X_f$, and once no constraint is active the MPC with this terminal cost is the LQR, whose value drops by exactly $q$ at every step. The theorem requires only the inequality; the example attains it.

**Inside the terminal set the MPC is the LQR.** On 200 random states of the full-motor $\mathcal X_f$, $J_5^\star$ matches $x^\top Px$ to $3 \times 10^{-15}$ and the first input matches $-Kx$ to $10^{-14}$: from $x \in \mathcal X_f$ the LQR plan is admissible and optimal, because $P$ is a fixed point of the Riccati recursion of chapter [[a07]], so the upper bound $J_N^\star \le V_f$ holds with equality.

**Try it on the demo.** Switch to "no terminal constraint, cost $P$ only": from the reference start the loop still converges and the value falls to 49.917 on the first step, lower than design B's 50.164, because no terminal constraint restricts the plan; but its residual is positive on one of the eleven steps, which design B never allows. The numbers script runs this design with $N = 3$ from every grid start: it loses feasibility from 10 of them, for instance from $(-4, 3)$ after two feasible solves, and its value fails to drop by $q$ on some step from 86 of them. From $(-4, 3)$ design B is infeasible at once. The unconstrained-terminal design has the larger region, but no theorem stands behind it.

## What the theorem does and does not say {#theorem}

**It is a sufficient design mechanism.** The assumptions provide a constructive route to recursive feasibility and stability. They are sufficient conditions, not a claim that every stable MPC must contain exactly these ingredients. MPC without a terminal constraint may have a larger actual region of attraction, but characterising it is much harder, and chapter [[b06]] points to the literature that does it for long horizons.

**The zero terminal set is simple but conservative.** $\mathcal X_f = \{0\}$ makes the proof almost immediate and the upper bound an extra assumption, and it forces the finite horizon to do all the work: at $N = 5$ with the weaker motor its region is a quarter smaller than design B's, and it needs two more steps to fill $\mathcal C_\infty$.

**Increasing the horizon is another lever.** It enlarges the region at online cost; the ingredients carry information about the tail at none.

**Nominal versus robust.** The feasibility proof uses the predicted successor $x_1$ as the actual one. If disturbances or model errors move the real system elsewhere, the shifted sequence starts from a state it was not planned for and may no longer be feasible. The guarantee is a nominal MPC guarantee for the stated model and constraints; robust MPC needs constructions that account for uncertainty explicitly (exercise 8, and chapter [[b06]]).

**Nonlinear extension.** The same problem can be posed for $x_{k+1} = g(x_k, u_k)$ with the same terminal structure, and the logic survives: invariance gives the shifted feasible tail, terminal decrease gives the cost argument, and $J_N^\star \le V_f$ on $\mathcal X_f$ gives the upper bound when the origin is interior and $V_f$ is continuous with $V_f(0) = 0$. The hard part is constructive: a useful nonlinear invariant set and terminal Lyapunov function are much harder to compute than in the linear-quadratic case, which is why the chapter keeps the full construction linear.

## The code behind it {#code}

The terminal constraint enters the condensed QP as a few extra rows on the last block of the prediction matrices.

::: code advanced_mpc_lmpc/mpc.py:LinearMPC.solve
Read the block that starts with `if self.terminal_H is not None`. The last $n$ rows of $S_u$ and $S_x$ map the input sequence and the current state to the predicted terminal state, $x_N = S_x^{(N)} x_0 + S_u^{(N)} U$. A polytopic terminal set $H_f x_N \le h_f$ therefore becomes $H_f S_u^{(N)} U \le h_f - H_f S_x^{(N)} x_0$: a matrix that is fixed and a right-hand side that moves with $x_0$, exactly like the state rows above it. Design A is the same code with the four rows of the origin as a polytope, $\pm x_N \le 0$; design B passes the rows of $\mathcal X_f$. The terminal cost enters earlier, where `Qbar[-n:, -n:] = self.P` replaces the last $Q$ block of the stacked weight by $P$. Nothing else in the solve changes between the three designs, which is why the hero can switch between them at the price of a few rows.
:::

The admissible region of the local law is one intersection.

::: code advanced_mpc_lmpc/polytopes.py:closed_loop_admissible_set_scalar_input
Two rows, $-K$ and $K$, with right-hand sides $u_{\max}$ and $-u_{\min}$, encode $u_{\min} \le -Kx \le u_{\max}$; intersecting them with the state box gives $\mathcal X_K$, the dashed parallelogram of the widget. The terminal set is then `maximal_invariant(A - B @ K, XK)` from chapter [[a10]], and the three conditions are checked at its vertices by the numbers script.
:::

## Common pitfalls {#pitfalls}

1. **"Feasible now means recursively feasible."** No. A feasible plan can end in a state from which the next optimisation has no feasible continuation; the terminal construction exists to prevent this, and chapter [[a06]] shows it happening.
2. **"A long horizon automatically proves stability."** No. It can improve behaviour and enlarge the region, but "large enough" is not a Lyapunov proof unless further conditions are established.
3. **"$P$ from the DARE is enough by itself."** Not under constraints. The terminal cost decreases as required while the LQR action violates the actuator limits; hence the admissible invariant terminal region, and the cost-only design's lost feasibility from $(-4, 3)$.
4. **"The terminal set must be the origin."** No. The point of the general theorem is to replace $x_N = 0$ by a nontrivial invariant region, and the upper bound comes free with it.
5. **"The solver objective is the Lyapunov value."** Not in software. QP implementations drop constants; for diagnostics, reconstruct the complete cost.
6. **"The theorem covers the plant."** It covers the model. With mismatch the line "$x(1) = x_1$" of the proof fails, and the guarantee with it.

## Check your understanding {#check}

:::: qa
Try to answer each question before opening it.

::: q 1. State the three general terminal assumptions.
A positive-definite stage cost; a terminal set invariant under an admissible local controller; a terminal cost that decreases under that controller by at least the local stage cost.
:::
::: q 2. What sequence is used in the shifted-sequence proof, and which assumption makes it feasible?
$(u_1^\star, \dots, u_{N-1}^\star, \kappa_f(x_N))$: the old plan minus its first move plus one move of the local law. Condition 2 makes the appended move admissible and keeps the new terminal state in $\mathcal X_f$; the earlier states were feasible already.
:::
::: q 3. What are the roles of the terminal set and the terminal cost?
The set supplies a feasible infinite tail through an invariant local controller, hence recursive feasibility; the cost supplies a Lyapunov bound on the cost of that tail, hence the decrease.
:::
::: q 4. Why is the decrease inequality alone not a proof of asymptotic stability?
With the lower bound it gives convergence, but Lyapunov stability needs an upper bound near the origin so that small starts keep the value, and the state, small. With $x_N = 0$ this needs local controllability with small inputs; with a terminal set containing the origin in its interior it follows from $J_N^\star \le V_f$ on $\mathcal X_f$.
:::
::: q 5. Why use the DARE solution as terminal cost?
With the LQR feedback it satisfies the exact decrease identity $V_f(x^+) - V_f(x) = -q(x, -Kx)$, the terminal Lyapunov condition with equality, and inside $\mathcal X_f$ it makes the MPC equal to the LQR.
:::
::: q 6. Why compute an invariant terminal set after computing the LQR?
LQR stabilises the unconstrained system, but its input may exceed the actuator bounds. The invariant terminal set restricts operation to a region where the state and the LQR action remain admissible indefinitely.
:::
::: q 7. Why does a nonzero terminal region enlarge feasibility relative to $x_N = 0$?
The horizon only has to reach a state from which the local controller can safely continue, rather than the exact equilibrium: area 20.1 against 15.1 at $N = 5$ with the weaker motor.
:::
::: q 8. Why does the hero's value fall by almost exactly the stage cost?
Once the plan ends inside $\mathcal X_f$ the tail term is zero by the DARE identity, and once no constraint is active the MPC is the LQR, whose value drops by exactly $q$. The theorem needs only the inequality.
:::
::: q 9. How is the feasible set of design B related to chapter A9's sets?
It is the $N$-step controllable set to $\mathcal X_f$, $\mathcal K_N(\mathcal X_f)$, nested in $N$ because $\mathcal X_f$ is control invariant, and contained in $\mathcal C_\infty$.
:::
::: q 10. What does the theorem say from a start outside $\mathcal X_N$?
Nothing. The first problem is infeasible and the controller has no plan; that is the honest answer, and the alternative designs that accept the start carry no guarantee.
:::

Each exercise has a hint and a solution; the code runs with the course package installed. Try first, then peek.

::::: exercises
:::: exercise The general proof, line by line
Derive $J_N^\star(x_1) - J_N^\star(x_0) \le -q(x_0, u_0^\star)$ keeping the terminal terms, and mark where each of the three conditions is used.
::: hint
Write the candidate's cost at $x_1$ with its own terminal term $V_f(x_{N+1})$.
:::
::: solution
$J_N^\star(x_0) = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} q(x_i, u_i^\star) + V_f(x_N)$. The shifted candidate is feasible at $x_1$ by condition 2, so by optimality $J_N^\star(x_1) \le \sum_{i=1}^{N-1} q(x_i, u_i^\star) + q(x_N, \kappa_f(x_N)) + V_f(x_{N+1})$. Subtracting, $J_N^\star(x_1) - J_N^\star(x_0) \le -q(x_0, u_0^\star) + [q(x_N, \kappa_f(x_N)) + V_f(x_{N+1}) - V_f(x_N)]$, and the bracket is at most zero by condition 3 with $x_{N+1} = Ax_N + B\kappa_f(x_N)$. Condition 1 is not used here; it is what turns the decrease into convergence in [[#lyapunov|Part 2]].
:::

:::: exercise The zero-terminal shifted sequence
Write the predicted state sequence at time $t$ for design A and construct the candidate at $t + 1$. Where exactly is the equilibrium assumption used?
::: hint
Follow the last predicted state one more step under the appended input.
:::
::: solution
At $t$: states $x_{t|t} = x(t), x_{t+1|t}, \dots, x_{t+N|t} = 0$ under $u_{t|t}^\star, \dots, u_{t+N-1|t}^\star$. Apply $u(t) = u_{t|t}^\star$, so nominally $x(t+1) = x_{t+1|t}$. Candidate at $t+1$: inputs $(u_{t+1|t}^\star, \dots, u_{t+N-1|t}^\star, 0)$, states $x_{t+1|t}, \dots, x_{t+N|t} = 0$ and then $x_{t+N+1} = A\cdot 0 + B\cdot 0$. The equilibrium assumption $A0 + B0 = 0$ is used exactly there, to conclude $x_{t+N+1} = 0$, and $0 \in \mathcal U$ is needed for the appended input to be admissible.

:::: exercise The upper bound from the terminal set
Prove $J_N^\star(x) \le V_f(x)$ for $x \in \mathcal X_f$, and say what the bound adds to the decrease.
::: hint
Apply $\kappa_f$ for $N$ steps and sum condition 3 along the plan.
:::
::: solution
For $x \in \mathcal X_f$ the plan $u_k = \kappa_f(x_k)$ is feasible by condition 2 applied $N$ times. Summing condition 3 along it, $\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} q(x_k, \kappa_f(x_k)) \le V_f(x_0) - V_f(x_N)$, so the plan costs at most $V_f(x_0)$, and the optimum costs no more. With $V_f = x^\top Px$ this gives $J_N^\star(x) \le \lambda_{\max}(P) \lVert x \rVert^2 = 1.911 \lVert x \rVert^2$ on $\mathcal X_f$. The decrease and the lower bound give $\sum_t \lVert x(t) \rVert^2 < \infty$, hence convergence; only the upper bound makes small initial states stay small, $\lambda_{\min}(Q)\lVert x(t) \rVert^2 \le J_N^\star(x(0)) \le \lambda_{\max}(P) \lVert x(0) \rVert^2$ for $x(0) \in \mathcal X_f$.
:::

:::: exercise Model mismatch
Suppose the real plant is $x^+ = (A + \Delta A)x + Bu$ while the optimiser uses $A$. Which step of the recursive-feasibility proof fails, and why does the nominal proof no longer suffice?
::: hint
Find the one line of the proof that says "under the nominal model".
:::
::: solution
The step "the system moves to $x_1$", the claim that the measured next state equals the predicted one. With mismatch, $x(1) = x_1 + \Delta A\, x(0)$, so the shifted candidate starts from a state it was not planned for: its intermediate states are no longer the old predictions, may leave $\mathcal X$, and its terminal state need not land in $\mathcal X_f$. Nothing in the nominal argument bounds the effect of $\Delta A$; feasibility at $x(1)$ is then a hope, not a theorem. Robust MPC repairs this by planning for the whole set of states the mismatch can produce, for instance by tightening the constraints by a tube around the nominal prediction, which chapter [[b06]] outlines.

## The chapter on one page {#summary}

::: steps
- **Three conditions**: positive-definite stage cost; $\mathcal X_f$ invariant under an admissible $\kappa_f$; $V_f$ decreasing under $\kappa_f$ by at least $q$.
- **The shifted sequence** $(u_1^\star, \dots, u_{N-1}^\star, \kappa_f(x_N))$ proves recursive feasibility, and optimality against it proves $J_N^\star(x_1) - J_N^\star(x_0) \le -q(x_0, u_0^\star)$.
- **Stability** needs the lower bound (condition 1) and an upper bound near the origin; a terminal set gives $J_N^\star \le V_f$ on $\mathcal X_f$ for free, $x_N = 0$ needs local controllability instead.
- **LQ ingredients**: $(K, P)$ from the DARE with the exact identity, $\mathcal X_f$ the maximal invariant set inside $\mathcal X_K$: four vertices, two iterations, area 1.83 with the weaker motor.
- **Reach**: with the weaker motor at $N = 5$, areas 15.10 for $x_N = 0$ and 23.31 for $x_N \in \mathcal X_f$, against $\mathcal C_\infty = 30.29$; the designs fill it at $N = 12$ and $N = 10$.
- **The loop** from $(-3.5, -0.5)$: 12 steps, five saturated, value $50.16 \to 37.72 \to 23.60$, residual at most $10^{-14}$; inside $\mathcal X_f$ the MPC is the LQR to $10^{-14}$.
- **The fine print**: sufficient conditions, nominal model, no disturbances.
:::

### The vocabulary, in one table

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Terminal set, terminal cost, terminal controller | Where the plan must end; the price of ending there; the law that could take over there. |
| Shifted sequence | Old inputs minus the first, plus one local move: the feasibility witness. |
| Recursive feasibility | Feasible now implies feasible after the applied move. |
| Class-$\mathcal K$ function | Continuous, strictly increasing, zero at zero. |
| Region of attraction | Starts from which the loop converges; here $\mathcal X_N = \mathcal K_N(\mathcal X_f)$. |
| $\mathcal X_K$, $\mathcal X_f$ | Where the LQR input is admissible; its maximal invariant subset. |
| Lyapunov residual | $J_N^\star(x^+) - J_N^\star(x) + q(x, u)$, non-positive by the theorem. |
| Weaker motor | The canonical cart with $|u| \le 0.5$, where the horizon lever is visible. |

**Scope.** Nominal MPC for linear systems with polytopic constraints and a measured state: the terminal-set and terminal-cost conditions, the zero terminal constraint as their special case, the linear-quadratic ingredients from the DARE and the maximal positively invariant set, and numerical checks of every inequality. An exact model and no disturbances are assumed; robust and output-feedback MPC are outside the scope, and the nonlinear case is discussed only qualitatively.
