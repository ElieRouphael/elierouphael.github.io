## The forbidden interval, and why the union is nonconvex {#geometry}

**The scene.** A vehicle of length $l_{\mathrm{car}} = 4.5$ m drives along a road towards a crossing that a bicycle will occupy; the conflict region is $w_{\mathrm{bike}} = 8$ m wide, centred at $d_{\mathrm{bike}} = 100$ m. The vehicle's centre is $d$, so it occupies $[d - l_{\mathrm{car}}/2,\ d + l_{\mathrm{car}}/2]$ and the region is $[d_{\mathrm{bike}} - w_{\mathrm{bike}}/2,\ d_{\mathrm{bike}} + w_{\mathrm{bike}}/2]$. Two ways not to overlap. Yield side: the front stays short of the near edge. Pass side: the rear has cleared the far edge. Hence

$$\boxed{d \le d_{\max} = d_{\mathrm{bike}} - \tfrac{l_{\mathrm{car}} + w_{\mathrm{bike}}}{2} = 93.75} \quad\text{or}\quad \boxed{d \ge d_{\min} = d_{\mathrm{bike}} + \tfrac{l_{\mathrm{car}} + w_{\mathrm{bike}}}{2} = 106.25},$$
%narrow \boxed{d \le d_{\max} = 93.75} \ \text{or}\ \boxed{d \ge d_{\min} = 106.25}

and the forbidden centre positions form a gap of $d_{\min} - d_{\max} = l_{\mathrm{car}} + w_{\mathrm{bike}} = 12.5$ m.

**Why the safe set is nonconvex.** During the bicycle's presence the collision-free set is $\mathcal S_{\mathrm{safe}} = (-\infty, d_{\max}] \cup [d_{\min}, \infty)$, the union of two half-lines. Take $d^{(1)} = 92.75$ and $d^{(2)} = 107.25$, both safe; their midpoint is 100, the middle of the crossing. A convex combination of safe points need not be safe, and that is the whole reason a logical decision appears: every tool of chapters [[a03]] to [[a05]] assumes the feasible set is convex, and a union of two convex pieces is not.

::: define What is a disjunctive constraint?
**In plain words:** a requirement of the form "this or that", where each alternative is easy but the choice between them is not, because the set of allowed states is two separate pieces.

**More precisely:** a constraint $x \in \mathcal A \cup \mathcal B$ with $\mathcal A, \mathcal B$ convex and disjoint. With a binary variable $s \in \{0, 1\}$ and a constant $M$ large enough, it becomes two linear inequalities, $d \le d_{\max} + M(1 - s)$ and $d \ge d_{\min} - Ms$, and the problem a mixed-integer quadratic program. With one binary variable it is solved exactly by solving the two convex alternatives and taking the cheaper feasible one.

- Yield, pass :: Stay short of the region; be beyond it, while the bicycle is present.
- Big-M :: A constant that switches an inequality off when its branch is not chosen.
- MIQP :: Quadratic objective, linear constraints, some integer variables.
- Relaxation :: The same problem with $s \in [0, 1]$ instead of $\{0, 1\}$: a QP whose optimum bounds the MIQP's from below.
:::

## One decision variable, two linear rows {#binary}

Fix the strategy in advance and each problem is a convex QP: linear dynamics, linear state and input constraints, a convex quadratic objective, plus $d_k \le d_{\max}$ during the bicycle's presence for yield, or $d_k \ge d_{\min}$ for pass. The nonconvexity appears only when the optimiser is asked to choose between them.

**The Big-M encoding.** Let $s = 1$ mean yield and $s = 0$ pass. The symmetric form is

$$\boxed{d_k \le d_{\max} + M(1 - s), \qquad d_k \ge d_{\min} - Ms},$$

which relaxes the branch not chosen by $M$: for $s = 1$ the first row is the yield constraint and the second reads $d_k \ge d_{\min} - M$, void if $M$ is large enough; for $s = 0$ the roles swap. With a quadratic objective and a binary $s$ this is a mixed-integer quadratic program [[ref:2]]. A compact one-sided variant, $(1 - s)\, d_{\min} \le d_k \le d_{\max} + (1 - s) D$ with a large $D$, encodes the same logic with one constant and the knowledge that positions are non-negative.

## The smallest valid M, and what a huge one costs {#bigm}

**Valid.** "Void" means satisfied by every position the car could possibly be at. If the positions over the horizon lie in a known envelope $[d_{\mathrm{low}}, d_{\mathrm{high}}]$, the switched-off rows need $d_{\mathrm{high}} \le d_{\max} + M$ and $d_{\mathrm{low}} \ge d_{\min} - M$, so

$$\boxed{M \ge \max(d_{\mathrm{high}} - d_{\max},\ d_{\min} - d_{\mathrm{low}})}.$$

For positions in $[0, 300]$ m that is $M \ge \max(206.25, 106.25) = 206.25$; for $[0, 150]$ it is $106.25$. Too small an $M$ invalidates the formulation: a switched-off row would still cut feasible plans, and the hero turns the relaxed set orange to show it. The lab's round $M = 5000$ is valid, and 24 times larger than needed.

**Why a very large M is not free.** A mixed-integer solver does not enumerate; it solves the relaxation with $s \in [0, 1]$, uses its optimum as a bound, and branches on $s$ only where the bound is not conclusive. Drag the hero's $s$ to 0.5: for any valid $M$ the relaxed rows allow $d \in [d_{\min} - M/2,\ d_{\max} + M/2]$, which contains the whole crossing, so the relaxation can place the car in the middle of the bicycle and report a cost that no real plan attains. The larger $M$, the looser the relaxation, the weaker the bound, the more branching; large constants also worsen the conditioning of the QP and make integrality tolerances troublesome. Production hybrid MPC derives the smallest valid bounds from the state envelope, as [[#exercises|Exercise 2]] does; the satellite course's chapter 09 meets the same weakness in a scheduling MILP.

## Two QPs instead of a MIQP {#enumerate}

There is exactly one binary decision, so the mixed problem is

$$\min\{J^\star_{\mathrm{yield}},\ J^\star_{\mathrm{pass}}\},$$

with infeasible branches discarded: solve the yield QP, solve the pass QP, keep the cheaper feasible one. Transparent, solver-independent, and exactly equivalent to enumerating the binary variable, with no $M$ anywhere. From lab [[b01]]'s nominal state $(60, 5)$ with $N = 4$ and $\mu = 0.10$, yield is feasible at cost 2582.56 and ends at $(85.886, 8.924)$, while pass is infeasible, so yield is selected; the yield bound is not even active, since the closed-form reachable terminal positions are $[74.11, 85.89]$ and neither bound is within reach, so the "decision" was made by the dynamics. With more grip, $\mu = 0.15$, from $(30, 17)$ both branches are feasible, and only the weights decide: at $R = 0.1$ and $R = 1$ passing is cheaper, at $R = 1000$ yielding, which can coast, wins. **When enumeration stops scaling.** With $m$ independent binary decisions there are $2^m$ branches: 8 for three, 1024 for ten, a million for twenty. A mixed-integer solver's pruning, by the relaxation bounds, is what makes such problems tractable, and the quality of the bounds, hence the tightness of $M$, is what decides how much pruning happens. The rule: one or two decisions, enumerate; dozens, formulate with the smallest valid constants and hand the problem to a branch-and-bound solver.

## One binary per manoeuvre, not per step {#manoeuvre}

If the bicycle occupies the region over several steps, say $k = 4, 5, 6$, the yield constraint must hold at every one of them, and so must the pass constraint; one strategy variable for the whole window commits to one topological manoeuvre: near side throughout, or far side throughout. Independent binaries $s_k$ at every step would let the optimiser be yield-side at one conflict instant and pass-side at a later one, which physically means driving through the forbidden interval while the bicycle is there. From $(0, 19)$ with the constraint enforced only at step 4 the plan is feasible and passes $78.7, 98.7, 118.7$ m at steps 4, 5, 6, straight through the crossing; enforced over the window it is infeasible, as it should be. Logical variables must encode the manoeuvre-level logic, not individual inequalities. Lab [[b01]] builds the window version and maps where it bites.

## The code behind it {#code}

The package writes the branch QPs of lab [[b01]] without any $M$: the strategy is an argument, and only its rows differ.

::: code advanced_mpc_lmpc/collision.py:solve_collision_strategy
Read only the block headed "Conflict exclusion at selected prediction steps". For each conflict step $k$, `idx` picks the position row of the predicted state $x_k = S_x x_0 + S_u U$ (chapter [[a02]]'s matrices), so `row` is the position's dependence on the inputs and `offset` its free motion. Yield appends $d_k \le d_{\mathrm{yield}}$ as `row @ U <= d_yield - offset`; pass appends $d_k \ge d_{\mathrm{pass}}$ with the signs flipped. Everything above those lines, the speed-tracking cost and the speed bounds, is shared by both branches, and everything below hands one convex QP to `solve_qp` with the friction limit as input bounds. The `conflict_steps` argument is the window of [[#manoeuvre|Part 5]]; its default, the last step only, is the single-instant version.

## Common pitfalls {#pitfalls}

1. **"The double-integrator dynamics make the problem convex."** No. Each fixed strategy is a convex QP; the union "$d \le d_{\max}$ or $d \ge d_{\min}$" is not. The logic, not the plant, creates the nonconvexity.
2. **"An enormous Big-M can only help."** Too small invalidates the formulation; needlessly large weakens the relaxation, slows the search and degrades the numerics.
3. **"The relaxation's optimum is a plan."** At a fractional $s$ the relaxed rows allow the car inside the crossing; the relaxation gives a bound, not a trajectory.
4. **"One binary per step is more flexible."** It is wrong: it allows a plan that is on the near side at one conflict instant and the far side at the next, through the bicycle.

## Check your understanding {#check}

:::: qa
Try to answer each question before opening it.

::: q 1. How are $d_{\max}$ and $d_{\min}$ derived?
By preventing overlap between the vehicle interval $[d - l_{\mathrm{car}}/2, d + l_{\mathrm{car}}/2]$ and the conflict interval $[d_{\mathrm{bike}} - w_{\mathrm{bike}}/2, d_{\mathrm{bike}} + w_{\mathrm{bike}}/2]$. The two no-overlap cases give $d \le d_{\mathrm{bike}} - (l_{\mathrm{car}} + w_{\mathrm{bike}})/2$ or $d \ge d_{\mathrm{bike}} + (l_{\mathrm{car}} + w_{\mathrm{bike}})/2$.
:::
::: q 2. Why is the collision-free set nonconvex?
It is a union of two separated half-lines. Two safe points on opposite sides have an unsafe midpoint inside the forbidden interval.
:::
::: q 3. What do the two Big-M rows do at $s = 1$ and at $s = 0$?
At $s = 1$ the first row is the yield constraint and the second is void; at $s = 0$ the second is the pass constraint and the first is void. "Void" requires $M$ to cover the whole position envelope.
:::
::: q 4. What is the smallest valid $M$, and what happens below it?
$\max(d_{\mathrm{high}} - d_{\max}, d_{\min} - d_{\mathrm{low}})$ for positions in $[d_{\mathrm{low}}, d_{\mathrm{high}}]$; below it a switched-off row still excludes feasible plans.
:::
::: q 5. Why can we solve two QPs instead of a MIQP here?
There is one binary decision. Enumerating $s = 0$ and $s = 1$ gives exactly the minimum of the two branch-optimal costs, with no $M$ and no relaxation.
:::
::: q 6. What is the main danger of a large Big-M in a mixed-integer solver?
It weakens the continuous relaxation, whose bound is what lets branch-and-bound prune; a weaker bound means more branching, and large constants also hurt conditioning and tolerances.
:::
::: q 7. Why one binary for the whole occupancy window?
Because the decision is a manoeuvre, near side or far side throughout; per-step binaries would permit crossing the forbidden interval between two conflict instants.
:::

::::: exercises
:::: exercise Conflict geometry
Derive $d_{\max}$ and $d_{\min}$ from interval non-overlap, then show $d_{\min} - d_{\max} = l_{\mathrm{car}} + w_{\mathrm{bike}}$.
::: hint
Two intervals do not overlap when one ends before the other begins.
:::
::: solution
No overlap means $d + l_{\mathrm{car}}/2 \le d_{\mathrm{bike}} - w_{\mathrm{bike}}/2$ or $d - l_{\mathrm{car}}/2 \ge d_{\mathrm{bike}} + w_{\mathrm{bike}}/2$, which rearrange to $d \le d_{\max}$ and $d \ge d_{\min}$. Subtracting, $d_{\min} - d_{\max} = 2 \cdot (l_{\mathrm{car}} + w_{\mathrm{bike}})/2 = 12.5$ m. `conflict_bounds()` returns the pair.
:::
::::
:::: exercise Big-M
Starting from $s = 1$ for yield and $s = 0$ for pass, derive the symmetric Big-M constraints, and for a known envelope $d \in [d_{\mathrm{low}}, d_{\mathrm{high}}]$ derive valid lower bounds on $M$. Evaluate them for $[0, 300]$ and $[0, 1000]$.
::: hint
The relaxed inequality must be satisfied by every position in the envelope.
:::
::: solution
Yield must hold when $s = 1$ and be void when $s = 0$: $d_k \le d_{\max} + M(1 - s)$. Pass must hold when $s = 0$ and be void when $s = 1$: $d_k \ge d_{\min} - Ms$. "Void" means satisfied by every $d \in [d_{\mathrm{low}}, d_{\mathrm{high}}]$: $d_{\mathrm{high}} \le d_{\max} + M$ and $d_{\mathrm{low}} \ge d_{\min} - M$, so $M \ge \max(d_{\mathrm{high}} - d_{\max},\ d_{\min} - d_{\mathrm{low}})$. For $[0, 300]$ that is $\max(206.25, 106.25) = 206.25$; for $[0, 1000]$ it is $906.25$.
:::

:::: exercise The relaxation
With $M = 206.25$, write the relaxed set of positions allowed at $s = 0.25$, $0.5$ and $0.75$. At which $s$ does the relaxed set first contain the whole crossing?
::: hint
The set is $[d_{\min} - Ms,\ d_{\max} + M(1 - s)]$.
:::
::: solution
$[54.7, 248.4]$, $[3.1, 196.9]$ and $[0, 145.3]$ (clipped at the road's start). The crossing $[93.75, 106.25]$ is contained as soon as $d_{\min} - Ms \le 93.75$ and $d_{\max} + M(1 - s) \ge 106.25$, that is $s \ge 0.061$ and $s \le 0.939$: almost any fractional $s$ lets the relaxation sit in the crossing, which is why its bound is weak. The numbers script prints the five sets.

:::: exercise Weights against feasibility
From $(30, 17)$ with $\mu = 0.15$, solve both branches for $R = 0.1$, $1$ and $1000$. Does feasibility change? Does the choice?
::: hint
`choose_collision_strategy` with `mu=0.15` and `Ru` varied; the widget's second and third presets.
:::
::: solution
Both branches are feasible every time, with yield costs 220.80, 226.88, 1563.44 and pass costs 36.69, 41.76, 5165.38: the choice is pass, pass, yield. The weights change which feasible plan is cheapest and never whether a plan exists; feasibility is set by the dynamics and the constraints alone.

```python
for Ru in (0.1, 1.0, 1000.0):
    best, sols = choose_collision_strategy(np.array([30.0, 17.0]), N=4, mu=0.15, Ru=Ru)
    print(Ru, [s.success for s in sols], [round(s.objective, 2) for s in sols], best.strategy)
```

:::: exercise Build it: the enumeration
Write your own `choose(x0)` that calls `solve_collision_strategy` for both branches and returns the cheaper feasible one, then count, on the grid $d_0 = 0, 5, \dots, 95$ and $v_0 = 0, 1, \dots, 32$, how many states have no feasible branch at $\mu = 0.10$.
::: hint
660 states, 1320 QPs; `strategy_grid` does the same and is the check.
:::
::: solution
Seven states have no feasible branch, along the line $d_0 + 4v_0 \approx 100$: too fast to stop short, too slow to clear. `strategy_grid` returns the same count; lab [[b01]] draws the map and repeats it at lower friction.

```python
def choose(x0):
    sols = [solve_collision_strategy(x0, s, N=4, mu=0.10) for s in ("yield", "pass")]
    feasible = [s for s in sols if s.success]
    return min(feasible, key=lambda s: s.objective) if feasible else None
none = sum(choose(np.array([d, v])) is None for d in range(0, 100, 5) for v in range(0, 33))
count, _ = strategy_grid(np.arange(0, 100, 5), np.arange(0, 33), N=4, mu=0.10)
print(none, (count == 0).sum())
```

## The chapter on one page {#summary}

::: steps
- **Geometry**: safe centre positions are $d \le 93.75$ or $d \ge 106.25$, a nonconvex union with a 12.5 m gap; the midpoint of two safe points is unsafe.
- **One binary**: $d_k \le d_{\max} + M(1 - s)$ and $d_k \ge d_{\min} - Ms$ encode the disjunction; the problem becomes an MIQP.
- **The smallest valid $M$** is $\max(d_{\mathrm{high}} - d_{\max}, d_{\min} - d_{\mathrm{low}})$: 206.25 for positions within 300 m; 5000 is valid and 24 times too large.
- **A huge $M$ costs**: at a fractional $s$ the relaxation allows the car inside the crossing, so its bound is weak and branch-and-bound prunes less.
- **Enumerate**: with one decision, two QPs are the exact solver; from $(60, 5)$ only yield is feasible, cost 2582.56; from $(30, 17)$ with more grip the weights pick the branch.
- **One binary per manoeuvre**: per-step binaries would drive through the crossing between two conflict instants.
:::

### The vocabulary, in one table

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Conflict region, forbidden interval | Where the bicycle will be; the centre positions that would overlap it. |
| Disjunctive constraint | "Either here or there": a nonconvex union of convex sets. |
| Yield, pass | Stay short of the region; be beyond it, during the occupancy. |
| Big-M, MIQP | A large constant switching inequalities off; the resulting mixed-integer program. |
| Relaxation | The binary allowed to be fractional; its optimum is a bound. |
| Enumeration | Solving every branch as a convex problem and keeping the cheapest feasible one. |

**Scope.** One logical decision on a one-dimensional point mass, lab [[b01]]'s model. Mixed-integer solvers, branch-and-bound and cutting planes are not covered; the satellite course's chapters 08 and 09 run them on a scheduling problem.
